## Bottom line

TX RRC stayed fully dark again this cycle — 56 days behind, unchanged counts across all three plays — so every genuine signal this cycle came from Louisiana's live permit feed, the Enverus lease-point exports, and the corporate layer. Two things stand out. First, this cycle's real newsmaker is the Permian royalty-trust fight, not a drilling story: SoftVest–Blackbeard's $2.24B PBT combination finally got a shareholder-meeting date (November 23) and an effective S-4, but SoftVest's own Voting and Support Agreement — the commitment behind getting that meeting called in the first place — has been quietly terminated, which makes the November 23 vote considerably less of a formality than the "meeting finally scheduled" headline suggests. Second, the leasing data is doing exactly what it's supposed to do — running ahead of permits — and it's surfacing real competitive intelligence the permit leaderboards can't: Sage Natural Resources is building Lee County leasehold at ten times its current permit pace, Sabine Energy and the RFE family are converting East Texas leasing volume directly into the TX Haynesville permit lead they already hold, and a cluster of unfamiliar fund-named entities (Lone Peak Energy, Incline Permian III, Black Swan Permian II, Espuela Energy) are leasing hard across Reeves/Upton/Winkler with zero matching permit footprint and zero findable corporate history — worth a direct name-check before they either show up as new permit filers next cycle or turn out to be the land side of a deal not yet public. Underneath both, the two confirmed foreign-LNG-capital positions in the Haynesville (JERA's closed $1.5B South Mansfield buy, Comstock-SOCAR's $1.65B deal now two days from its own self-imposed deadline) remain the clearest throughline connecting this pipeline's permit data to the macro LNG-feedgas story driving Haynesville activity in the first place.
