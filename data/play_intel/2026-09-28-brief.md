## Haynesville (Texas + Louisiana)

Combined TX+LA permit history shows **289 Haynesville permits** (158 TX / 131 LA) against the standard Haynesville county/parish footprint, unchanged from last cycle on the TX side (frozen master) and flat on the LA side (the two fresh LA permits both land outside the play). The last live lease-filing snapshot (9/21) is now a week old but still the freshest read available.

**Comstock–SOCAR has moved from letter of intent to a signed framework agreement — as of today.** Comstock and SOCAR (Azerbaijan's state oil company) signed a **Framework Agreement on September 28, 2026** — announced at the Azerbaijan International Investment Forum — formalizing the $1.65B strategic partnership first announced 9/1. The framework memorializes both parties' commitment to negotiate and execute a **definitive purchase and sale agreement by no later than October 31, 2026**, with the transaction still guided to **close by year-end 2026**. Comstock retains operational control of all upstream assets and stays operator of Pinnacle Gas Services; the two companies will also jointly explore SOCAR marketing Comstock's gas as international LNG. This sits alongside the separate $450M Jones-family drilling venture (85%/80% D&C funding on 18 Western + 9 Legacy Haynesville wells, effective 9/1) that's already running. Comstock's TX permit book remains concentrated in Harrison/Panola/Rusk (8 permits per the full county sweep, 24 counting the tracked-family alias including LA), and per the 9/27 intel-insight read, Comstock's in-corridor DOXA/Sabine exposure is smaller than headline counts suggest — only 2 of 7 TX client-watch permits (Bryant Rains BMP/EB, Harrison) sit in that corridor itself, with the other 5 in the separate West Haynesville watch list (3 Leon, 1 Robertson, 1 Freestone).

**Apex (Citadel-backed) remains the play's dominant lessee and one of its most active drillers, and now shows up directly under its own name in the LA permit book, not just leases.** Citadel bought Paloma Natural Gas for $1.2B in Feb 2025, renamed it Apex Natural Gas, then paid Comstock $430M in Dec 2025 for additional LA/TX acreage. Apex has run as many as 13–16 rigs at points this year and was described as the most active Haynesville operator by early 2026; Haynesville-wide rig counts crossed 60 for the first time in 32 months earlier this month, led by Apex's build-out. That tracks with the evidence: **Apex Natural Gas, LLC carries 29 LA Haynesville permits — the #1 tracked-family filer in that state** (De Soto 48, Caddo 35, Red River 15, Bossier 14 lead LA's parish breakdown overall) — plus 5 TX permits, and led Caddo Parish's 165-lease total in the last live digest (147 of 165) and all 18 of Nacogdoches County's. National gas-directed rig counts hit their highest level since July 2023 in the week ending 9/25 (135 rigs, +1 w/w), consistent with LNG-driven Haynesville activity outrunning oil-basin momentum right now.

**Mitsubishi's Aethon deal is closed and the Aethon→Adamas rename is fully visible in the permit book.** Mitsubishi's ~$7.5B (per its own release; other coverage frames it as ~$5.2B equity/$2.33B assumed debt) acquisition of Aethon's Haynesville business closed 7/15/2026. `config.yaml` tags this **"Adamas (ex-Aethon, Mitsubishi)."** TX master shows Adamas with 12 Haynesville permits, LA shows 11 (Bienville, Red River, Bossier, Natchitoches) — the #2 tracked-family filer in LA behind Apex. Per the 9/27 aging read, Adamas's LA book includes Bienville's WW Farms pair and 3-well Raley pad, Bossier's 3-well Cowley pad, two Natchitoches singles and one Red River single, spread 39–116 days unspudded.

## Eagle Ford / Eaglebine (Giddings)

Quieter by volume — 363 Eagle Ford/Eaglebine permits in the TX master history — but this is the play with the biggest live news this cycle.

**BP explored a multibillion-dollar bid for Devon Energy's Eagle Ford assets, then walked away — confirmed by reporting dated 9/24–9/25, i.e. inside this brief's own coverage window.** BP was evaluating $2–5B of U.S. shale acquisitions, including Devon's South Texas Eagle Ford package (TPH Research pegs it around $4.5B). BP entered Devon's data room — BPX Energy has ~15 years of Eagle Ford operating history from its former Blackhawk Field joint venture with Devon, dissolved in early 2025 — but multiple sources now report **BP has backed away from the deal**; entering a data room never guaranteed a formal bid. Devon is still marketing both its Eagle Ford and Powder River (Wyoming) acreage as part of the portfolio review that followed its $58B all-stock Coterra merger (closed 2026, the same deal discussed under Permian below). **Watch this one for a different buyer** — Devon's stated intent to exit Eagle Ford hasn't changed even though this specific suitor has. BPX Operating Company itself is already active on the ground independent of any Devon deal: 18 TX Eagle Ford permits on file and a top-5 lessee in the DLS/EOG Giddings corridor.

**Magnolia's WildFire acquisition is closed and confirmed, unchanged from last cycle.** Magnolia Oil & Gas announced completion September 10, 2026; WildFire owners received 32.2M shares of Magnolia Class A stock and Magnolia assumed WildFire's $600M of 2029 notes. The deal adds ~810,000 net acres and pushes Magnolia's combined Giddings position past 1.25M net acres across the Austin Chalk, Eagle Ford, and Woodbine. Magnolia carries **20 permits in the tracked DLS/EOG Giddings corridor** (Fayette/Lee/Washington) — per the 9/27 aging read, all 20 are still unspudded (a 6-well Cyclone pad in Fayette, even-numbered wells Two through Twelve, at 65–69 days; Clay Creek-adjacent clusters at 40–81 days) — the newly-combined company inherits a substantial pre-close DUC-adjacent inventory.

**A cross-play Aethon/Adamas wrinkle, still live.** Separate from the Haynesville-side Adamas rename above, **Aethon Energy Operating LLC still carries 18 active Eagle Ford/Eaglebine permits in Washington County** under the legacy Aethon name (the Clay Creek East/West clusters, per the 9/27 read now 25–33 days unspudded) — outside the Mitsubishi transaction's stated Haynesville scope, unconfirmed via search whether that's a deliberate carve-out or simply a different asset. Either way, the tracked "Adamas (ex-Aethon)" family is running under two names in two plays at once, exactly the naming drift `config.yaml`'s alias list exists to catch.

**ConocoPhillips still runs the legacy Eagle Ford core through two names post-Marathon.** The $22.5B all-stock Marathon acquisition (closed 2024) grew ConocoPhillips's Eagle Ford net-location count 85%; Burlington Resources O&G Co LP (30 permits) and Marathon Oil EF LLC (15 permits) both keep filing separately under the same ultimate ownership. Per the 9/27 aging read, Marathon's **Cherry Bergey Selman SA4 in Gonzales County has now sat unspudded for 653 days** (issued December 2024) — still the single oldest live permit in either state's book.

**EOG remains the play's historical anchor** — 46 permits, the largest single Eagle Ford operator on file, and also the largest tracked-family operator TX-wide (63 permits combined across plays). Its Eagle Ford leasing runs in parallel with Permian activity (17 tracked Permian permits), not instead of it.

**ExxonMobil's absence remains a confirmed divestiture story, not a data gap.** XTO (Exxon's subsidiary) confirmed 1/23/2026 it is marketing ~168,000 net acres and 1,000+ Eagle Ford wells for roughly $1B, part of Exxon's broader ~$15B divestment program. No named buyer has surfaced yet.

**Smaller names on schedule:** Ironbloom Operating (1 permit, Lee County) remains the tracked "new Giddings entrant." Sage Natural Resources holds 4 permits (Lee/Fayette), no fresh leasing since the 9/21 batch. Crescent Energy's broader Eagle Ford roll-up (Ridgemar ~$905M, SilverBow ~$2.1B) still hasn't surfaced a named entity in permit evidence.

The Permian still dominates raw volume — 1,297 of TX's 2,037 master rows (76% of MTD issuance per today's market brief) against the standard Permian county footprint. National rig count rose to 599 (+4) for the week ending 9/25; the Permian itself gained one rig, while Eagle Ford lost one — a reminder that this cycle's Eagle Ford story (BP/Devon) is strategic, not yet showing up in activity.

**Continental Resources' acquisition of Firebird Energy II — the user's own tracked family — is still guided to close this month, but we found no confirmation it has actually closed as of today.** Announced 8/20/2026: Continental will acquire Firebird Energy II's ~54,000 net Midland Basin acres (147,000 net resource acres, ~95% operated, ~32,000 boe/d, 69% oil, 307 gross development locations) from Quantum Capital Group. Every source still reads "expected to close September 2026, subject to customary closing conditions" — no completion press release turned up in today's search. Since September ends this week, **the next cycle should specifically check for a close announcement.** The underlying pattern flagged last cycle still holds: Continental (43 historical TX Permian permits) and Firebird (21 permits, all tracked-family) are both filing inside the same six-county DOXA/Firebird corridor right now — 12 of Firebird's are still unspudded per the 9/27 read, split across two Ector pads (Pocket Pair, 6 wells, 88 days; Jake P, 6 wells, 30–44 days) that Continental will inherit on close.

**The two largest Permian megadeals in shale history still sit under the top two names in the permit data.** ExxonMobil's $59.5B Pioneer acquisition (closed May 2024) has Exxon's Permian output at a Q2 2026 record above 1.8 MMboe/d, targeting 2.5 MMboe/d by 2030; Pioneer Natural Resources USA remains TX's largest single operator (124 permits). Diamondback's $26B Endeavor merger (closed 2024) put Diamondback at #2 (110 permits).

**Devon–Coterra's June 2026 close is still driving capital allocation, and is the same deal behind this cycle's Eagle Ford story.** The all-stock Devon–Coterra combination (reported as ~$58B in this week's BP coverage) sends more than 60% of the combined ~$4.9B 2026 capital plan to the Permian. Coterra Energy Operating Co. keeps filing under the legacy name (109 permits, heavily Culberson/Reeves — the Delaware Basin side) — TX's #3 filer. The portfolio review that produced this week's BP/Eagle Ford story is a direct consequence of this merger: Devon is simplifying around the Permian by shedding Eagle Ford and Powder River.

**Targa Resources' Permian midstream build-out continues.** Targa's $1.25B acquisition of Stakeholder Midstream (Permian gathering, treating, processing, and crude gathering assets across ~170,000 dedicated acres) was guided to close Q1 2026 — background context on how much midstream capital keeps following the upstream activity in this data, alongside the previously-flagged SoftVest/Blackbeard $2.24B "PBT Land and Minerals" combination (still pending a unitholder vote, guided to close H2 2026).

**Apache/APA is running a steady LNG-linked program rather than chasing M&A** — 47 historical TX permits, and a distinctive ~$300M/year Cheniere LNG marketing contract tying Permian gas directly to export pricing, a small preview of the same LNG-linked capital theme dominating Haynesville this cycle.

**Double Eagle's build-scale-sell cycle and the untracked Reagan/Martin clusters (Oxyrock's Martin County THREE PENNY/THREE pads, DE Central Operating's Reagan-heavy 51-permit book) are unchanged from last cycle** — no new search hits this round, still worth a periodic second look given their scale.

## Bottom line

The single most consequential development this cycle is dated **today**: Comstock's Haynesville partnership with SOCAR has moved from a letter of intent to a **signed framework agreement**, with a definitive PSA targeted for October 31 and closing guided by year-end — a concrete, near-term milestone the user should track directly rather than treat as background LNG-capital color. Layered against that, this cycle surfaced and largely resolved a new Eagle Ford thread in the same few days: BP explored and then walked away from a $2–5B bid for Devon's Eagle Ford package, confirming Devon's post-Coterra-merger intent to exit the play (and its former partner's continued interest in the basin) without a deal actually landing — worth a light watch for whichever buyer comes next. Continental's acquisition of the user's own tracked Firebird family is still the one item to chase hardest: it was guided to close "this month," this month ends in two days, and no closing announcement has surfaced yet. The structural caveat repeated for a fourth straight cycle is unchanged and now more acute: TX permit visibility has been frozen at a 9/4 snapshot for 24 days under an RRC IP block now in its 12th day, and the Enverus lease export has gone quiet for a full week (9/22–9/28) rather than the three days flagged last cycle — real activity (SOCAR's framework signing, BP's Devon pass, Continental/Firebird's imminent close) is outrunning what this pipeline's own feeds can confirm same-day by a widening margin.
