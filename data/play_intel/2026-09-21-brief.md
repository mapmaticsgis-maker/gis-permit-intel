## Haynesville (Texas + Louisiana)

This is the loudest play in the evidence again this cycle. It's the single largest category in today's lease-filing export — **216 of 720 new leases tagged HAYNESVILLE-LA, plus 81 more HAYNESVILLE-TX (297 of 720, 41% of everything filed)** — and combined TX+LA permit history shows **264 Haynesville permits** (133 TX / 131 LA) against the standard Haynesville county footprint. The macro story underneath it is unchanged from last cycle and keeps compounding: foreign, LNG-linked capital is buying into this play faster than almost anywhere else in U.S. shale.

**Comstock just added a third capital partner on top of last cycle's Jerry Jones financing.** Two weeks ago this brief flagged Comstock's $450M Jones-family drilling venture (85% of D&C costs on 18 Western Haynesville wells, 80% on 9 Legacy wells, effective 9/1/2026). New this cycle: on the same day, Comstock also announced a **$1.65B strategic partnership with SOCAR** (State Oil Company of Azerbaijan) — SOCAR buying a 20% non-operated interest in Comstock's Legacy Haynesville assets, 15% of Western Haynesville (stepping down to 7.5% after five years), and 15% of Comstock's 73% stake in Pinnacle Gas Services. That's now three distinct capital sources (Jones family, SOCAR, and Comstock's own balance sheet) funding the same 545,000-net-acre Western Haynesville push. Comstock's permit book remains concentrated in Harrison/Rusk/Panola per `tx/master.csv` (24 permits, still TX's #1 Haynesville filer), and yesterday's intel-insight brief flags a real backlog behind that number: **23 unspudded Sabine Energy permits** (a separate tracked name, not Comstock) in the same corridor, ranging 18–492 days, topped by the Hill CV A/B pair in Panola sitting since May 2025.

**Apex (Citadel-backed) is the dominant lessee in the play, full stop.** Citadel bought Paloma Natural Gas for $1.2B in February 2025 and renamed it Apex Natural Gas; in December 2025 Apex paid Comstock $430M for additional LA/TX Haynesville acreage — the flip side of Comstock's Western Haynesville pivot above. That shows up starkly in today's lease digest: **Apex leased 183 new tracts (2,735.9 ac) this cycle alone**, including **147 of Caddo Parish's 165 total leases** and 18 of Nacogdoches County's 18. Apex is also LA Haynesville's #1 permittor in the full master history (29 permits, Caddo/Red River-heavy), and yesterday's aging-inventory read shows Apex's Kuhn cluster in Caddo (4 permits, 114 days) as the corridor's oldest standing cluster — acreage assembly running well ahead of spuds.

**Mitsubishi's Aethon deal is fully closed, and the Aethon→Adamas rename is visible across both permits and leases simultaneously.** Mitsubishi closed its ~$5.2B equity / $2.33B assumed-debt acquisition of Aethon Energy's Haynesville business on July 15, 2026 (Aethon retains a 25% non-operated interest and is repositioning as a broader investment manager). `config.yaml` already tags this tracked family as **"Adamas (ex-Aethon, Mitsubishi)."** Our evidence shows the transition mid-flight in real time: Adamas Energy LLC carries 12 TX Haynesville permits and 11 LA Haynesville permits (23 combined) plus 2 aging Camp-lease wells in the West Haynesville watch corridor (109 days), while this week's *lease* filing under the legacy name still reads **AETHON III HV LLC — 1 new lease, Natchitoches Parish, 20 ac.** Same family, two names, both active in the same week's data.

**Expand Energy (Chesapeake+Southwestern) remains the single biggest capital commitment in the play by dollars.** Now the largest U.S. gas producer (7.48 Bcf/d in Q2 2026), Expand is putting more than half its full-year 2026 capex into the Haynesville (745,000 net acres, a 22-year drilling inventory). This week's lease digest shows **Expand Operating LLC / Expand Gulf Coast LLC with 20 new leases (441.7 ac)**, concentrated in De Soto (9 leases) and Caddo (2) Parishes — smaller in raw count than Apex this cycle but consistent, ongoing leasehold assembly. Yesterday's pad data adds two standing Expand clusters in Caddo (a 3-well pad permitted 9/15, a 2-well pad permitted 8/31) plus 8 aging unspudded permits (16–113 days) in the NW Louisiana corridor.

**JERA's closed South Mansfield deal is showing up as fresh leasehold this exact week — a name `config.yaml` doesn't yet track.** JERA (the Japanese utility JV) closed its $1.5B acquisition of 100% of the South Mansfield upstream asset from Williams Upstream Holdings and GEP Haynesville II, LLC in February 2026. **GEP Haynesville** is the #2 lessee in Natchitoches Parish this week (11 leases) and appears in both Sabine (6) and Red River (4) Parishes too — real, current leasehold activity from a name outside the tracked `operator_families` list. Between Mitsubishi's Aethon buy and JERA's South Mansfield deal, well over $6.7B of Japanese, LNG-linked capital has entered this play in the last eight months; worth adding GEP Haynesville to the tracked-families list given the volume it's now showing.

**Two similarly-named Haynesville operators trace back to two different owners of the same legacy asset — worth not confusing them.** Tokyo Gas's TG Natural Resources (TGNR) bought the original Rockcliff Energy for $2.7B (closed Jan 2024) — **TGNR Panola LLC (9 permits) and TGNR East Texas II LLC (6 permits)** are that entity's active TX filers. Separately, a *new* Rockcliff Energy III LLC was formed in 2024 backed by Quantum Capital Group and, in October 2025, acquired Pine Wave's Shelby Trough East Texas Haynesville assets — that's **ROCKCLIFF ENERGY OPERATING III, 10 permits**, a different company under different ownership despite the shared name. Both are active in the same DOXA/Sabine corridor this cycle.

**BPX (BP) remains the quiet, steady name in this play** — 10 LA Haynesville permits on file, no new leasing signal in this week's digest, consistent with BP's broader posture of running a single Haynesville rig and prioritizing capital productivity over new acreage.

## Eagle Ford / Eaglebine (Giddings)

Quieter than Haynesville by volume — **48 leases tagged EAGLEFORD plus 65 EAGLEBINE (113 combined) in this week's digest**, against 368 Eagle Ford permits in the TX master history — and that's consistent with outside coverage describing Eagle Ford M&A as "muted relative to other U.S. shale basins" through a multi-year stretch, with competition from the Permian a structural drag on deal flow here.

**Magnolia's $4.06B WildFire deal is the play's defining story, and it's about to close.** Magnolia Oil & Gas agreed in July 2026 to acquire WildFire Energy for ~$4.06B, adding roughly 810,000 net acres and 53,000 boe/d and more than doubling Magnolia's Giddings position to 1.25M+ net acres — pro forma, nearly 80% larger than the #2 operator in the trend. The deal has full board approval and was expected to close **late Q3 2026, i.e. essentially now.** Our evidence catches both companies drilling side-by-side in the same corridor in the same window: Magnolia carries 20 permits in the DLS/EOG Giddings corridor (Fayette/Lee/Washington) — and yesterday's aging list shows all 20 still unspudded, 33–74 days — while **WildFire Energy Operating LLC** shows a single permit in Lee County in today's 90-day corridor rollup. Once the deal closes, that WildFire permit and any pending leasehold fold directly into Magnolia's program.

**ConocoPhillips runs the traditional Eagle Ford core through two legacy operating names.** ConocoPhillips's $22.5B all-stock acquisition of Marathon Oil closed in 2024 and grew ConocoPhillips's Eagle Ford net-location count by 85%. **Burlington Resources O&G Co LP (30 permits)** and **Marathon Oil EF LLC (15 permits)** are both still filing as separate entities under the same ultimate ConocoPhillips ownership — Burlington is also actively seeking RRC approval for new horizontal wells in DeWitt and Karnes Counties this cycle. Worth flagging: ConocoPhillips is separately reported working with advisers on potential Eagle Ford and Permian asset sales to trim debt from the Marathon deal — a possible explanation if either legacy book thins out in coming cycles.

**EOG remains the play's historical anchor** — 46 permits, the largest single operator in the full TX Eagle Ford history, consistent with EOG being the play's original developer and with `config.yaml`'s own framing of the Giddings corridor as "EOG-contiguous." EOG is also this cycle's #1 watched-family lessee statewide per today's market brief (11 new leases, 1,092.2 ac, split between Pecos, Lavaca, and DeWitt Counties) — meaning EOG's Eagle Ford leasing is running alongside its Permian activity, not instead of it.

**ExxonMobil's absence from this play remains a signal, not an omission.** Exxon began marketing roughly $1B of Eagle Ford position for sale in early 2026 — exactly why the company doesn't appear anywhere in our Eagle Ford operator lists: right now it's a seller here, not a driller.

**Smaller names worth tracking:** Javelin Energy Partners (a Crescent Energy subsidiary, 15 historical permits in the corridor) sits inside a broader Crescent Energy roll-up strategy — Crescent separately closed ~$905M for Ridgemar Energy's Eagle Ford assets and $2.1B for SilverBow Resources, plus $47.9M and $309.9M of Eagle Ford mineral/royalty portfolio purchases in Jan/Feb 2026 — none of those specific deal-entities appear yet in our permit evidence, plausibly because they're too recent to show up in filings; worth checking upcoming cycles for Crescent's name to surface directly. **Ironbloom Operating** (1 permit, Lee County) remains `config.yaml`'s tracked "new Giddings entrant" and continues to show up right on schedule. **Sage Natural Resources** (4 permits, Lee/Fayette) added 4 new leases this week (403.9 ac, Fayette).

## Permian Basin

The Permian dominates raw permit volume in every cut of this evidence — 76% of TX's month-to-date issuance per today's market brief, 1,275 of 2,037 TX master rows against the standard Permian county footprint, and 111 MIDLAND + 35 DELAWARE + 10 CENTRAL BASIN PLATFORM + 2 NORTHWEST SHELF = **158 of this week's 720 new leases** — but the operator roster underneath those numbers is now almost entirely a list of the last three years' Permian mega-mergers.

**The two largest Permian deals in shale history sit directly under the top two names in the permit data.** ExxonMobil's $59.5B acquisition of Pioneer Natural Resources (closed May 2024) combined Pioneer's Midland Basin core with Exxon's existing Delaware/Midland position — 1.4M+ net combined acres, an estimated 16 billion boe resource, a path toward ~2 MMboe/d of Permian output by 2027 via long (up to 4-mile) laterals. **Pioneer Natural Resources USA remains the single largest operator in the full TX master history (124 permits, 118 in the trailing 90-day "permit banking" read, 64 within the tracked DOXA/Firebird corridor alone)** — still filing under the Pioneer name, now running Exxon's program. Diamondback Energy's $26B merger with Endeavor Energy Resources (closed 2024) combined the Permian's 7th- and 9th-largest producers into a ~$50B, 838,000-net-acre operator concentrated in the Midland sub-basin — **Diamondback E&P LLC carries 110 historical permits (75 in the trailing 90-day read, 50 in the Firebird corridor)**, the state's #2 filer.

**Devon's merger with Coterra closed in June 2026 — new this cycle, and it's now driving the play's capital allocation.** Devon Energy completed its all-stock merger with Coterra in June, and the combined company's 2026 capital plan (~$4.9B) sends **more than 60% of full-year spend to the Permian.** Wells are still filing under the legacy name: **Coterra Energy Operating Co. carries 109 historical TX permits**, the state's #3 filer, with new completion designs reportedly delivering some of the highest oil cuts in the combined portfolio's book.

**A modest caveat on one entity we can't fully confirm this cycle:** **DE Central Operating LLC** shows 51 historical permits (12 this month per the prior cycle's read) concentrated in the Delaware/Central Basin Platform footprint — the name strongly suggests a legacy Double Eagle vehicle (last cycle's brief connected a related "DE V Operating LLC" to Diamondback's $4.08B purchase of Double Eagle IV's Midland Basin assets and the subsequent Double Eagle V/Tumbleweed V $2.5B relaunch), but this cycle's search didn't turn up a source confirming the "DE Central" name specifically — flagging as probable, not confirmed, same caveat as last cycle.

**Apache (APA Corp) is running a steady, LNG-linked program rather than chasing new M&A.** APA averaged five Permian rigs in Q2 2026 (four in the Southern Midland Basin, one Delaware), raised its full-year U.S. oil outlook to 122,000 bbl/d, and carries a distinctive Cheniere LNG marketing contract worth ~$300M/year in international gas pricing exposure — a rare direct Permian-to-LNG capital linkage outside the Haynesville. APA's 2024 Callon Petroleum acquisition remains the basis for its current ~4,000-well, 2.6M-gross-acre position; our evidence shows 47 historical TX Permian permits (21 in the Firebird corridor: Midland, Upton).

**Midstream is building ahead of all of this,** which reads as confirmation of sustained multi-year commitment rather than a one-off: ONEOK is acquiring Brazos Midstream's Midland Basin gathering system, and separately, the SoftVest/Blackbeard combination with Permian Basin Royalty Trust ($2.24B, forming "PBT Land and Minerals") is consolidating Central Basin Platform minerals into a new public vehicle even as Blackbeard Operating LLC keeps drilling in the meantime (14 permits in the trailing 90-day Firebird-corridor read, a four-well Davy Jones/Waddell Tr cluster in Crane County per yesterday's pad data).

**Tracked families:** none of the user's tracked `operator_families` carry material direct Permian volume this cycle except EOG (6 MTD permits, statewide top watched-family). Notably, **Firebird's own DOXA/Firebird corridor shows Firebird itself quiet** — 21 historical permits total, all 12 of Ector County's Firebird permits sitting unspudded in yesterday's aging read (23–81 days, the Jake P and Pocket Pair leases) rather than converting to fresh activity, while third parties (Diamondback, Pioneer, Blackbeard, Ring Energy) are the ones actively drilling around Firebird's own position in that corridor. Separately worth flagging: Oxyrock filed 14 permits across two adjacent Martin County pads in a single day (9/2) — Martin isn't in any tracked corridor, and Oxyrock isn't a tracked family, but it's the single largest one-day cluster in the whole TX snapshot and worth a second look if the pattern repeats.

## Bottom line

The through-line across all three plays this cycle is the same one flagged last week, and it keeps getting stronger: capital is re-rating gas over oil, and the Haynesville is where that shows up hardest. Comstock alone now has three separate outside capital partners (Jones family, SOCAR, and its own balance sheet) funding the same Western Haynesville push, Apex (Citadel) leased 183 new tracts in a single week, and JERA's now-closed South Mansfield deal is visible as fresh GEP Haynesville leasehold this exact week — a name worth adding to the tracked list given the volume. The Permian and Eagle Ford stories, by contrast, are both about consolidation into fewer, larger hands (Exxon/Pioneer, Diamondback/Endeavor, Devon/Coterra, ConocoPhillips/Marathon, Magnolia/WildFire) rather than new capital entering the play — Devon/Coterra's June close and its >60%-to-Permian capital plan is the one genuinely new mega-deal signal this cycle. If one thing here warrants a client conversation this week, it's the RRC data gap itself: TX permit visibility has now been frozen at a 9/4 snapshot for 2.5 weeks running, with no active-rig data available from any source in this pipeline's history — real activity (like this week's 297 new Haynesville lease filings) is happening well ahead of what TX's own permit feed can currently confirm.
