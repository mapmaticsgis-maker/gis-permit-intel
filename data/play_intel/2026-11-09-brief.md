The underlying permit and lease data has not moved an inch since the 11/05 cycle — TX stayed dark for a seventh straight cycle (66 days behind), LA logged zero new permits, and the ingestion pipeline itself appears to have stopped firing entirely for four days — but this cycle's corporate research closed out real open questions and surfaced two structural facts this brief series had been missing outright. First, Comstock's SOCAR partnership converted from letter-of-intent-adjacent uncertainty to a signed, dated definitive agreement on November 4 — the clearest resolution of any open item this cycle, and the clock now runs toward a year-end close. Second, two of the three plays' single largest permit holders turned out to be major-company subsidiaries hiding behind legacy operator names that RRC's frozen feed has no mechanism to update: "Pioneer Natural Resources USA" (124 Permian permits, the largest single position in this dataset) is ExxonMobil, closed since May 2024, and "Marathon Oil EF" (15 Eagle Ford permits) is ConocoPhillips, closed since November 2024 — on top of the already-known Coterra/Devon and now a newly-confirmed Civitas/SM Energy pattern. That's four separate mega-merger legacy names sitting unflagged in RRC-sourced operator fields, a reminder that "who's permitting" and "who actually owns the permit" are now two different questions across a meaningful share of Permian and Eagle Ford volume. Third, Magnolia's abrupt CEO health-related transition — landing mid-integration on its ~$4.1B WildFire acquisition — is the one genuinely time-sensitive people-risk item in this cycle and worth a direct flag to the client rather than waiting for the next brief. Underneath all three plays, the foreign-capital thread keeps compounding: a newly-complete count puts four distinct, confirmed Japanese positions in the Haynesville alone (Mitsubishi/Adamas, JERA, Tokyo Gas/TGNR, and the long-standing but previously under-emphasized Osaka Gas/Sabine, which also happens to be carrying the single largest unspudded permit backlog on the TX side), plus Mitsui's quiet Eagle Ford lease position and HighPeak's still-unconfirmed Indonesian-backed Permian financing — all arriving ahead of, not in reaction to, the LNG-driven supply tightness Expand Energy flagged publicly this cycle.
