## Haynesville (Texas + Louisiana)

Combined TX+LA history against the standard Haynesville county/parish footprint: **173 TX permits** (led by Panola, Harrison, San Augustine, Rusk) and **139 LA permits** in the tracked "RROG + DOXA — NW Louisiana Haynesville" corridor (De Soto 50, Caddo 36, Red River 15, Bossier 15, Sabine 11). The TX side is the frozen 9/4 snapshot; LA is current through yesterday.

**Mitsubishi's Aethon acquisition has closed, and the Aethon→Adamas rename is now directly visible across both states' permit books.** Mitsubishi agreed in January to acquire Aethon Energy's Haynesville business for $5.2B in equity (~$7.5B including assumed debt) — Mitsubishi's first direct entry into U.S. shale gas across the full value chain — and operations have transitioned to **Adamas Energy LLC**, a new standalone subsidiary led by CEO Gordon Huddleston. `config.yaml` tags this family **"Adamas (ex-Aethon, Mitsubishi)."** The evidence shows the handoff mid-stream: **Adamas Energy LLC** carries 8 TX Haynesville permits (trailing 90 days) and 11 LA permits (Bienville, Bossier, Red River, Natchitoches, per yesterday's intel-insight read, 42–119 days unspudded) under the new name, while **Aethon Energy Operating LLC** still carries 16 active permits under the legacy name — but in Washington County, inside the Eagle Ford/Giddings corridor, not Haynesville (Clay Creek West/East units, 28–36 days old; see Eagle Ford section). Lease filings tell the same story in miniature: the 9/21 batch shows only 1 new Adamas-named lease (Natchitoches, 20 ac) against Aethon's historical leasehold — Mitsubishi bought an existing position rather than leasing fresh.

**Apex Natural Gas (Citadel-backed, ex-Paloma) is the play's dominant lessee and a top-tier driller on both sides of the state line.** Citadel bought Paloma Natural Gas for $1.2B in February 2025 and renamed it Apex Natural Gas; Apex then paid Comstock $430M in December for additional Haynesville acreage in both states, and separately bought Azul Resources' NW Louisiana position. `config.yaml` tags this **"Apex (ex-Paloma, Citadel)."** The evidence: Apex Natural Gas, LLC leads the LA corridor at **29 permits** — the single largest tracked-family filer in either state's Haynesville book — and the 9/21 lease digest shows Apex with **183 new leases (2,736 ac)**, dominated by **147 of Caddo Parish's 165 total leases** and all 18 of Nacogdoches County's. Yesterday's intel-insight read puts Apex's unspudded LA book at 13 wells across Caddo/Red River, 36–124 days old.

**JERA's closed South Mansfield acquisition now shows up directly under JERA's own name.** JERA closed its $1.5B purchase of the South Mansfield Haynesville asset (from Williams and GeoSouthern affiliates) in February 2026, acquiring a position producing 500+ MMcf/d with 200 undeveloped locations and a stated goal of reaching 1 Bcf/d; the deal sits alongside JERA's broader Gulf Coast LNG offtake strategy (Cameron LNG, the Blue Point ammonia project, and a 5.5 MMtpa, 20-year US LNG offtake deal). **JERA Upstream America, LLC** carries 4 LA permits in De Soto Parish (the Larkspur well, per intel-insight) and was the only non-watched-family-labeled buyer to appear fresh in the last 30 days of permit activity (1 permit, 9/15).

**Expand Energy (Chesapeake+Southwestern, closed 2024) is running the single most active drilling program in the play and is the only name carrying real 30-day momentum in this data.** Company guidance for 2026 calls for 11–12 rigs and $2.75–2.95B of capex, with more than half going to Haynesville; Expand drilled a second Western Haynesville well this year and added 33,000 Haynesville acres in Q2. In the evidence, **Expand Operating LLC** carries 26 LA permits and is the clear leader of the most recent 30 days — **8 of the last 17 LA permits issued** (through 9/29) are Expand's, spread across Caddo and Sabine parishes, plus 20 fresh leases (442 ac) in the 9/21 batch, 9 of them in De Soto.

**Mitsui E&P USA is a new, independent entrant in the Western Haynesville, operating alongside — not through — Comstock.** Mitsui bought a 75% working interest in 46,500 net acres of Western Haynesville leasehold (Leon and Freestone counties) from Sabana Haynesville and Vanna Production, and operates that acreage itself. The evidence shows 3 new Mitsui E&P leases in Freestone County in the 9/21 batch, alongside Comstock's own 54 leases in the same county — two separate operators building the same western edge of the play in parallel.

**Comstock Resources is mid-transaction with a second major LNG-linked partner: SOCAR.** Comstock and Azerbaijan's state oil company signed a **framework agreement on September 28 — three days before this brief** — advancing their $1.65B strategic partnership first floated earlier this year. The framework commits both sides to a definitive purchase-and-sale agreement by October 31, with closing guided for year-end 2026; Comstock retains operatorship and the two will jointly evaluate LNG offtake arrangements. On the ground, Comstock is simultaneously **doubling its Western Haynesville well count to 24 in 2026** (from 12 in 2025) and advancing a NextEra data-center power deal, even as it sold Haynesville acreage to Apex in December. In the evidence, Comstock's combined Freestone/Leon/Robertson leasing (121 TX leases across the two counties in the 9/21 batch) is the largest single-operator leasing position outside Caddo Parish, and its TX permit book (7 trailing-90-day, split Harrison/Leon/Robertson/Freestone) spans both the legacy East Texas corridor and the new western extension.

**BPX (BP) and Sabine Energy round out the top tier with no new M&A this cycle.** BPX Operating Company carries 10 LA permits with no fresh leasing signal — consistent with BP running a lean, steady Haynesville program while its strategic attention is on Eagle Ford and Permian divestitures (see below). Sabine Energy Inc. remains TX's single largest Haynesville filer by raw count (17 trailing-90-day, 24 all-time) with the play's largest unspudded backlog — 23 wells split Panola/Harrison/Rusk, 28–502 days old; the 502-day Panola pair is the second-oldest live permit in either state's evidence.

**Diversified Energy (tracked family) made a move that doesn't yet show up in this evidence.** Diversified closed its $245M purchase of Sheridan Production Partners' East Texas gas assets in April 2026 — contiguous acreage in Cherokee, Harrison, Nacogdoches, Panola and Rusk counties, the same core as the DOXA/Sabine corridor. Diversified's acquire-and-produce model means it isn't a new-permit filer, so neither Diversified nor Sheridan appears in `tx/master.csv` or the lease export; worth a standing watch given the direct overlap with the user's tracked corridor.

Haynesville rig activity, reported but not in this pipeline's own data: gas-directed rig counts in the play reportedly more than doubled from 22 (February 2025) to 52 (mid-February 2026), and federal forecasters are cited as expecting Haynesville to add more new U.S. gas supply than any other shale play through 2027.

## Eagle Ford / Eaglebine (Giddings)

TX history against the standard Eagle Ford footprint: **353 permits**, led by EOG Resources (46), Burlington Resources (30), Magnolia Oil & Gas (20), BPX (18), Aethon Energy Operating (16). Top counties: Webb, Dimmit, Karnes, DeWitt, Atascosa, with Washington leading the Giddings extension.

**ConocoPhillips now runs the legacy Eagle Ford core under two separate names, and the underlying merger closed six days ago.** ConocoPhillips' $17.1B all-stock acquisition of Marathon Oil — adding Eagle Ford, Bakken and Permian Delaware assets and roughly 2,000 drilling locations — **completed September 25, 2026**, with Marathon Oil exiting the public market. Eagle Ford is explicitly the center of this deal: it's roughly half of the combined new inventory, and the combination is described as creating a premier Eagle Ford operator at ~380,000 boe/d. In the evidence, **Burlington Resources O&G Co LP** (ConocoPhillips' existing Eagle Ford legacy brand, 30 permits all-time, DeWitt-centered) and **Marathon Oil EF LLC** (15 permits, Gonzales-centered, 71–656 days unspudded per intel-insight — the 656-day Cherry Bergey Selman pair is the single oldest live permit in either state's entire dataset) are now the same ultimate owner, filing separately with the ink barely dry on the deal.

**Magnolia Oil & Gas's WildFire Energy acquisition more than doubles its Giddings position and directly connects two names already sitting side-by-side in the corridor data.** Magnolia agreed in July to acquire WildFire Energy for $4.06B, adding roughly 810,000 net acres across Brazos, Burleson, Fayette, Grimes, Lee, Madison, Milam, Robertson and Washington counties and pushing Magnolia's combined Giddings position past 1.25 million net acres across the Austin Chalk, Eagle Ford and Woodbine. Magnolia is already the top tracked-family filer in the DLS/EOG Giddings corridor (20 permits, Fayette/Lee/Washington, all unspudded per intel-insight, 43–84 days old), and **WildFire Energy Operating LLC** already shows up as a separate filer in the same corridor (1 permit, Lee County) and in the West Haynesville watch list (2 permits, Robertson County) — both positions Magnolia will absorb once the deal closes.

**BP explored and then walked away from a bid for Devon's Eagle Ford package — a live, days-old story directly relevant to BPX's standing position.** Reporting from September 24–25 says BP was evaluating $2–5B of U.S. shale acquisitions including Devon Energy's South Texas Eagle Ford assets, but ultimately did not proceed. This sits inside BP's broader restructuring: BPX Energy (BP's US onshore arm, active in Permian, Eagle Ford and Haynesville) sold non-controlling interests in its Permian and Eagle Ford midstream assets to Sixth Street for $1.5B as part of BP's stated $20B divestment program through 2027 — BPX remains operator throughout. In the evidence, BPX Operating Company carries 17–18 Eagle Ford permits (trailing 90-day/all-time) with no fresh leasing signal, a steady-but-not-growing position consistent with a company selling equity stakes rather than drilling harder. Watch for a different Devon Eagle Ford buyer to surface — Devon's intent to exit hasn't changed even though this particular suitor passed.

**Smaller tracked names:** Sage Natural Resources (2 trailing-90-day permits, Fayette; also the top lessee in Fayette outside the dominant private "Flat Land O&G" filer, with the same Lee County lessor — Lonnie Lehmann — recurring four times at 101 acres each in the 9/21 batch) and Ironbloom Operating (1 permit, Lee County, the tracked "new Giddings entrant") both remain thin but present. Marathon Oil EF and FW Eagle Ford I round out the corridor's active drillers without any fresh M&A signal found this cycle.

## Permian Basin

TX history against the standard Permian footprint: **1,284 permits** — more than double either other play — led by Pioneer Natural Resources USA (124), Diamondback E&P (110), Coterra Energy Operating Co. (109), DE Central Operating (51), Apache (47). Top counties: Reeves, Midland, Andrews, Reagan, Martin — spanning both the Midland and Delaware basins.

**The two largest Permian megadeals in shale history still sit under the top two permit filers.** ExxonMobil's acquisition of Pioneer Natural Resources closed in 2024, doubling Exxon's Permian footprint; Exxon's Permian production is reported to have broken 1.8 MMboe/d in 2026. **Pioneer Natural Resources USA, Inc.** remains the single largest operator in the entire TX evidence set (124 all-time, 118 trailing 90-day, concentrated Andrews/Midland/Upton) — the legacy brand name continuing to file permits more than two years after the parent changed. Diamondback Energy's $26B merger with Endeavor Energy Resources created the largest pure-play Permian independent, with combined acreage across Midland, Ector, Glasscock, Howard, Andrews, Martin and Upton counties; **Diamondback E&P LLC** is the #2 filer (110 all-time, 75 trailing 90-day, Ector/Midland-centered), matching that exact county footprint.

**Devon–Coterra's all-stock merger of equals closed May 7, 2026, and the evidence shows the Coterra name cooling off in the most recent 90 days even as the combined company's capital plan leans harder into the Permian.** Devon issued approximately $24.8B in stock to acquire Coterra (0.70 Devon shares per Coterra share); the combined company trades as Devon Energy, is now the top Delaware Basin producer, and bought 16,300 net undeveloped federal Delaware acres within weeks of closing. **Coterra Energy Operating Co.** is TX's #3 all-time Permian filer (109 permits, overwhelmingly Culberson/Reeves — the Delaware Basin side) but doesn't crack the trailing-90-day top 8, a plausible post-merger integration pause five months after close — one to watch for whether permitting re-accelerates under Devon's combined capital program.

**ConocoPhillips' Marathon Oil close (Sept 25, six days ago) reaches into the Permian too, not just Eagle Ford.** The same $17.1B deal adds roughly 400 Permian Delaware locations to ConocoPhillips' existing book. **COG Operating LLC** — ConocoPhillips' existing Permian filer (45–46 permits, Andrews/Midland-centered, the #3 trailing-90-day filer) — is the entity that inventory lands under.

**Occidental's two legacy brands are both still active seven years after the Anadarko acquisition.** Occidental closed its ~$38B acquisition of Anadarko Petroleum in August 2019, adding roughly 250,000 Permian acres; **Anadarko E&P Onshore LLC** remains a wholly-owned Occidental subsidiary used as an active operating entity. In the evidence, Anadarko E&P Onshore LLC (25–33 permits, Loving/Reeves — Delaware Basin) and Occidental Permian Ltd. (24–32 permits) are both independently top-10 filers — the same ultimate parent, seven years post-close, still running two names in the same basin.

**Continental Resources' acquisition of the user's own tracked Firebird family is the single item most worth chasing next cycle.** Continental announced in August it will acquire FireBird Energy II (a Quantum Capital Group portfolio company) — roughly 32,000 boe/d, 147,000 net Permian resource acres, 54,000 net Midland Basin acres, 307 gross development locations, 69% oil — expected to close **September 2026**. September just ended; no closing announcement had surfaced as of this search. `config.yaml` tags this family **"Firebird Energy"** directly. In the evidence, Continental Resources (38–43 permits) and Firebird Energy II LLC (8–21 permits depending on window) are both filing inside the same DOXA/Firebird Permian corridor today — per yesterday's intel-insight read, Firebird's Ector County book carries 12 unspudded wells across three named pads (Pocket Pair 2715/2722, Jake P), 33–91 days old, that Continental will inherit the moment the deal closes. With TX permit visibility frozen at a 27-day-old snapshot, a close announcement is the kind of event this pipeline would not see land on its own.

## Bottom line

The dominant theme across all three plays this cycle is that the evidence is running behind a wave of consolidation that landed in just the last two weeks: ConocoPhillips–Marathon Oil closed September 25 (reshaping both Eagle Ford and Permian), Comstock–SOCAR moved from letter-of-intent to a signed framework agreement on September 28 with a named October 31 deadline, and Continental–Firebird is guided to close this month with no confirmation found yet — all three touch names already sitting side-by-side in this pipeline's own permit and lease data. Layered underneath that is a distinct, ongoing story: foreign LNG-linked capital is now a structural feature of the Haynesville specifically, not a one-off — Mitsubishi ($5.2–7.5B, closed, renamed Adamas), JERA ($1.5B, closed, South Mansfield), Mitsui (new operator, Western Haynesville), and now SOCAR (pending, $1.65B) have all bought or are buying directly into the play within roughly the last nine months, each tying upstream Haynesville gas to a Gulf Coast LNG offtake strategy. The structural caveat that should shape how all of the above is read: TX permit visibility has been frozen at a 27-day-old snapshot since RRC's IP block took effect on 9/17, and the Enverus lease export has now gone ten straight days without a new filing — real activity (the SOCAR framework, the Marathon close, whatever Continental announces on Firebird) is outrunning what this pipeline's own feeds can confirm same-day by a widening margin, and that gap is the thing most worth fixing before the next cycle.
