## Bottom line

For the second cycle running, every permit, rig, and lease signal this pipeline can see is frozen — TX's RRC feed has now been blind for 52 real days (39 days of confirmed bot-block), LA's master hasn't added a row since before 10/22, and the lease-point feed has gone six straight days without a new record. None of that means the plays themselves are quiet: it means this cycle's only genuine movement is in the corporate layer, and that layer moved in a way worth sitting with. The clearest lesson from today's research is methodological, not just substantive — Magnolia's $4.06B WildFire acquisition closed September 10, 2026, squarely inside its guided window, after two prior cycles of this same briefing reported "no closing confirmation found." That was a search-coverage failure, not a reporting gap on Magnolia's part, and it means Continental–FireBird's own three-cycle "unconfirmed" status (also now past its guided deadline) should be read as a flag to check SEC filings directly rather than evidence the deal fell through. Underneath that, the bigger structural fact for the Permian section is that "Coterra Energy Operating Co." — this dataset's third-largest Permian permit filer at 109, all Culberson/Reeves Delaware Basin acreage — has been a Devon Energy subsidiary since the two companies' $58 billion merger closed in May, which also explains the Eagle Ford exit (Devon's sale of ~90,000 Karnes/DeWitt/Gonzales net acres to Crescent Energy for ~$3.85B net) as portfolio consolidation rather than a standalone divestiture. And in the Haynesville, the Comstock–SOCAR $1.65B framework deal's self-imposed October 31 deadline for a definitive agreement is now 5 days away, with Comstock's own Q3 earnings call not landing until after it — the single most time-boxed item being tracked across all three plays this cycle.
