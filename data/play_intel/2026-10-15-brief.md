(24), Adamas Energy (12), RFE Operating (10), Rockcliff Energy Operating III (10), TGNR Panola (9), Silver Hill Energy Operating (8). **LA Haynesville: 154 permits, up from 143 on 10/12** — De Soto 61 (+11), Caddo 38, Red River 17, Bossier 15, Sabine 11, Bienville 5, Webster 4, Natchitoches 3. Leaders: Apex Natural Gas (31), Expand Operating (26), Comstock Oil & Gas–LA (15), BPX Operating (15, up from 12), Adamas (11), **WNR Haynesville Operating (8)**, **EXCO Operating (8)**, **JERA Upstream America (7)**. All 11 new De Soto permits landed inside a two-week pad-filing cluster detailed in `intel_insight`: BPX's Matthews TR7/TR8-18-19HC pad (3 wells, 10/6–10/7), EXCO's new Hall 1 HU pad (2 wells, 10/12-13), and WNR Haynesville Operating's three-well Petro-Hunt 13-12HC pad (10/13) — a pad `intel_insight` flags as genuinely three wells despite a stray-space naming glitch in SONRIS splitting it visually into 2+1. WNR is a small OKC-based operator (per Texas Drilling Co. records, 8 producing East Texas wells, Harrison/Panola) stepping up its De Soto filing pace; worth a name to watch given the sloppy internal land/GIS naming is itself a sign of an operator without an established GIS vendor relationship.

**JERA Upstream America's permits (7, Benson field, De Soto) are the direct, confirmed follow-through on JERA's $1.5B South Mansfield acquisition** — JERA (JERA Co., the Japanese utility) closed that purchase from Williams/GeoSouthern in February 2026 and has stated a goal of roughly doubling South Mansfield's >500 MMcf/d output toward 1 Bcf/d. These Legacy 10-3-34/1-3-34 HC permits are the first hard evidence in this pipeline's data of that drilling program actually moving.

**Comstock-SOCAR still has not reached a definitive agreement, and the October 31 deadline is now 16 days out.** Confirmed via fresh search: the only signed document remains the September 28 framework agreement (20% non-op stake in Legacy Haynesville, 15%→7.5% in Western Haynesville, 15% of Comstock's Pinnacle Gas Services stake, $1.65B cash, plus LNG-marketing cooperation). No signing of the definitive purchase-and-sale agreement found in any source checked today.

**The LNG-linked foreign-capital roster in this play now reads four deep, not three.** JERA ($1.5B, closed, now drilling per the permits above) and Mitsubishi ($7.5B Aethon/Adamas purchase, reported closed) are done deals; Comstock-SOCAR is the pending third. The fourth, newly relevant this cycle: **Mitsui E&P USA**, which leased 7 new parcels in Leon County in the 10/12 batch — this is not a new entrant but continuing activity under Mitsui's existing 2024 joint venture with Sabana Haynesville/Vanna Production (75% working interest, 46,500 net acres across Leon and Freestone counties, closed June 2024). Comstock is leasing the same Leon County ground in parallel (8 new leases from a single grantor, Cuatro Rojos LLC) — the same West Haynesville extension pattern flagged last cycle, now with Mitsui visibly active alongside it.

**Apex Natural Gas remains the play's most active operator on both the permitting and leasehold sides.** By the lease file's own DI Play tagging, this single 10/12 batch carried **194 new "Haynesville - TX" leases and 37 "Haynesville - LA" leases** — Apex alone accounts for 124 of the TX-side total (3,637 acres), concentrated 100-deep in Nacogdoches County alone, plus another 8 in Caddo and 8 in Red River Parish. Expand Exploration/Gulf Coast added 33 leases (3,427 acres) split between its established Anderson County (West Haynesville) program and new Bossier/De Soto Parish parcels. Sabine East Texas Basin picked up 7 leases (437 acres) in Harrison County — same corridor as its 24-permit TX-core lead.

**Diversified Energy** (tracked family) still shows nothing new: its $245M Sheridan Production East Texas deal (announced Feb. 26, 2026, ~75,000 net acres) remains unconfirmed as closed in any source found today, and no fresh leasing or permit activity appears in either feed.

**Basin-wide context, reconfirmed:** Haynesville gas output is reported on track for a new production record above 12.2 Bcf/d on continued Gulf Coast LNG feedgas demand, with at least one analyst view (KeyBanc, cited in trade coverage) that current drilling pace leaves the market undersupplied heading into 2027 — consistent with the drilling intensity visible in this cycle's lease and permit data.

## Eagle Ford / Eaglebine (Giddings)

**TX core (unchanged, frozen master): 287 permits** — Webb 72, Dimmit 29, Atascosa/DeWitt/Karnes 28 each, Gonzales 27. Leaders: EOG Resources (44), Burlington Resources (30), BPX (18), Javelin Energy Partners (15), Marathon Oil EF (15), Lewis Petro Properties (15). Plus **44** in the Eaglebine/Giddings extension, led by Magnolia (20) and Aethon Energy Operating (16).

**Crescent Energy's $4.2B agreement to buy Devon's entire Eagle Ford position (announced Oct. 8) gets fuller confirmation this cycle, including the headline-vs-net price gap.** Latham & Watkins' own release (it represented Crescent) and Crescent's Oct. 8 SEC 8-K both confirm the deal: ~90,000 net acres in Karnes, DeWitt, and Gonzales Counties, ~68,000 boe/d net production (55–60% oil), 600+ net Tier-1 locations normalized to 10,000-ft laterals, ~$140M in annual synergies, Devon mineral interests folding into Crescent Royalties (~$50M royalty EBITDA). The $3.85B figure is Crescent's own "net purchase price" framing; the $4.2B figure (Reuters, OGJ) is the gross cash number — not a discrepancy between sources, just net-of-adjustments vs. headline cash. Closing is guided for Q4 2026 or early 2027, funded from cash on hand plus a reported $1B follow-on equity offering. DeWitt and Gonzales are both counties where BPX, EOG, and Marathon Oil EF already carry permits in this corridor's leader list — Crescent becomes a direct neighbor to all three once it closes.

**This sits alongside Magnolia's own $4.06B purchase of WildFire Energy (announced July 2026, ~810,000 net acres across Eagle Ford and Austin Chalk)** — already the basis for Magnolia's 20-permit Eaglebine lead cited above — making Eagle Ford/Eaglebine consolidation a two-track story this year: Crescent rolling up Devon/Ridgemar/Vital in the core, Magnolia rolling up WildFire in the extension.

**EOG Resources** (tracked family) shows light but live leasing: 3 new leases (DeWitt 1, Lavaca 2, 22.1 acres total) in the 10/12 batch. By DI Play tag, this batch carried 9 "Eagleford" leases and 26 "Eaglebine" leases — the extension corridor is the more active leasing side of this play right now, consistent with Magnolia/Aethon's permit lead there.

**SM Energy's divestiture context is unchanged:** Caturus Energy's $950M Galvan Ranch purchase (closed April 30, ~61,000 net acres, Webb County) remains the only confirmed move; no fresh Caturus leasing this cycle. Silver Hill's January acquisition of 1776 Energy's ~17,500-net-acre Eagle Ford/Austin Chalk package is likewise unchanged, with nothing newer found today.

**Smaller tracked names — Sage Natural Resources (4 permits), Ironbloom Operating (1 permit) — show no new leasing this cycle.**

Both Midcontinent O&G and Cresta Exploration are the same unidentified high-volume lessees flagged in the prior cycle (510 and 217 cumulative leases respectively) as absent from that cycle's smaller file — they're back, confirming they're recurring, not one-off backlog entities, and still worth identifying directly.

**Inside the specific tracked DOXA/Firebird corridor (Ector, Winkler, Upton, Midland, Crane, Andrews), leasing stayed narrow despite the county-wide Midland surge above:** Crane 2 leases (SM Energy, Ring Energy — 16,933 acres combined), Ector 5 (SEP Land Fund), Upton 1 (Lone Peak Energy) — still no Winkler or Andrews entries, and the heavy Midland/Martin leasing sits outside this narrower corridor definition rather than inside it.

**Continental-FireBird still has not been confirmed closed, now more than a month past its guided September close.** No 8-K, press release, or SEC filing found today confirming completion of the ~147,000-net-acre, 32,000 boe/d FireBird Energy II purchase from Quantum Capital Group. A third-party deal-tracker entry (dated Aug. 26) claims it's done but gives no closing date or filing and conflicts with Continental's own later guidance — still the clearest "go check EDGAR directly" item in this brief, for a third cycle running.

**SoftVest-Blackbeard's $2.2B Permian Basin Royalty Trust combination has a clearer procedural picture now, if not a date.** New PBT Land and Minerals, Inc. has filed a Form S-4 (registration/proxy) and a separate S-1 for a unitholder rights offering; completion requires a Trust unitholder vote, and SoftVest separately petitioned to remove the Trust Indenture's 75% supermajority approval threshold (a hearing on that petition was set for May 8, 2026). Market guidance still points to a second-half-2026 close; no meeting date or closing date confirmed in today's search.

**HighPeak Energy's sale exploration is unchanged:** the Sept. 18 report of an international acquisition offer prompting a strategic review remains the only item on record — no buyer, no terms, nothing newer today.

**Devon-Coterra is confirmed closed, with an exact date.** SEC filings (Coterra's Q1 2026 10-Q, the joint DEFM14A) show both companies' shareholders approved the all-stock merger on May 4, 2026, with closing targeted for May 7, 2026 — consistent with the "closed Q2 2026" read carried in the prior brief, now dated precisely. Coterra Energy Operating Co. remains the #3 all-time TX Permian filer (109 permits) under the combined entity.

## Bottom line

LA is the only genuinely live permit feed this cycle, and it's handing over the clearest storyline: a De Soto Parish pad-filing cluster (BPX, EXCO, and new entrant WNR Haynesville Operating, all inside two weeks) sitting next to the first hard permit evidence — JERA Upstream America's 7 Benson-field filings — that JERA's $1.5B South Mansfield purchase is now translating into actual drilling, with Mitsui's continuing Leon County leasehold work rounding the LNG-linked foreign-capital roster in Haynesville out to four names. Separately, the lease-filing feed needed its own correction this cycle: what the last brief described as "October 10, 192 records" has been superseded by a backfill job that now shows the real last batch as October 12, 648 records — and that batch rewrites the read on which play is leasing hardest right now. It isn't Haynesville or Eagle Ford; it's the Permian, on the strength of a 183-lease Midland County surge that also re-surfaces both previously-flagged unidentified high-volume lessees (Midcontinent O&G, Cresta Exploration) still unidentified. Against all of that, both TX and LA permit *masters* carry forward two stale, unresolved items from prior cycles — Comstock-SOCAR's October 31 deadline is inside three weeks with still only a framework agreement, and Continental-FireBird has now missed its guided close by over a month with no confirmation anywhere — and the TX RRC block itself is now 28 days old with no lift in sight.
