## Bottom line

TX RRC stayed fully dark for a sixth straight Play Intel cycle — 62 days behind, every permit count across all three plays identical to 11/02 — but this cycle's LA pull was the first genuinely same-day read since 10/30, and it delivered real signal: Comstock's two-well Bienville infill is small in isolation, but it sits inside a Louisiana book (255 total rows, 171 Haynesville-corridor) whose composition is now well-explained end to end by this cycle's corporate research — Apex/Citadel on top by a wide margin, built from Paloma plus a Comstock asset purchase plus an Azul deal; Comstock itself holding the remainder it didn't sell; Expand running an 11-12 rig program with more than half its capex in-basin; JERA and Adamas/Mitsubishi both closed and stable; Centalion/WNR confirmed closed; only Comstock-SOCAR and Rockcliff III still genuinely open questions. Second, this cycle converted two "unconfirmed" items from 11/02 into primary-source-verified facts purely by checking the right filing: Continental's FireBird acquisition is now confirmed closed via its own 10-Q (not just a lapsed announcement), and Diamondback's Q3 2026 results — flagged last cycle as the single most immediate checkpoint — are in and show a dividend raise plus a new midstream equity stake. Third, and most useful for next steps: this cycle surfaced a plausible, previously-undrawn link between the Blackbeard/Greybeard entities drilling (8 Permian permits) and leasing (8 Permian leases) in Crane and Glasscock Counties and the same-named counterparty set to take ~40.7% of a new public company, PBT Land and Minerals, if Permian Basin Royalty Trust unitholders approve the SoftVest-Blackbeard combination at their November 23 meeting — the nearest hard date on the calendar across all three plays, and worth a direct follow-up the week it happens. Underneath all three plays, the foreign-capital thread keeps compounding rather than resolving: JERA's closed $1.5B Haynesville buy, Mitsubishi's closed (if value-unreconciled) Aethon/Adamas deal, Mitsui's quiet 6-lease Eagle Ford position, and HighPeak's still-unconfirmed $450M Indonesian-backed Permian financing — four separate foreign counterparties, the same underlying story of outside capital buying into U.S. shale ahead of LNG and power demand.
