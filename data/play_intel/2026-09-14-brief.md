## Haynesville (Texas + Louisiana)

This is the loudest play in tonight's evidence by a wide margin — it's the single largest category in the entire lease-filing export (**154 of 525 new leases tagged HAYNESVILLE-TX**, more than double the next play) — and it is where this cycle's biggest M&A story sits.

**Mitsubishi's $7.5B entry, and the Aethon→Adamas rename happening in real time.** Mitsubishi Corporation closed its acquisition of Aethon Energy's Haynesville business on July 15, 2026 — ~$5.2B in equity plus $2.33B of assumed debt, for assets straddling NW Louisiana and East Texas producing 2.1 Bcf/d. Aethon retained a 25% non-operated interest and is repositioning Aethon Energy Management as a broader energy-investment manager; Mitsubishi and Aethon also signed a non-binding agreement to explore LNG and data-center collaboration. This is exactly why `config.yaml` already tags this tracked family as **"Adamas (ex-Aethon, Mitsubishi)"** — and our own evidence shows the rename mid-flight: Louisiana permits are already filing under the new **ADAMAS ENERGY LLC** name (11 unspudded permits across Bienville/Natchitoches/Red River/Bossier per `la/master.csv`), while this week's Texas *leasing* is still closing under the legacy **AETHON UNITED BR LP** signature (3 new leases in Anderson County, 1,339.9 ac). Same family, two names, live in the data simultaneously.

**Citadel's Apex is the most aggressive driller in the play.** Citadel (the hedge fund) bought Paloma Natural Gas for $1.2B in February 2025 and renamed it Apex Natural Gas; in December 2025 Apex paid Comstock $430M for additional LA/TX Haynesville acreage. Apex has been running as many as 14 rigs and added over 1 Bcf/d of Haynesville growth since October 2025 (~1.4 Bcf/d by December). That shows up directly in our evidence: Apex Natural Gas is the #1 operator in LA Haynesville permits (**29** unspudded, Caddo/Red River-heavy) and the #1 tracked-family lessee in this week's TX filings (**22 leases, 2,199.6 ac, concentrated in Nacogdoches**).

**Comstock sold LA acreage to fund a Western Haynesville push — with fresh Jones-family financing two weeks old.** The Comstock→Apex $430M LA divestiture (above) is the flip side of Comstock's pivot into Texas's Western Haynesville extension (Robertson/Leon/Freestone/Houston/Madison — `config.yaml`'s own "West Haynesville watch" corridor). Comstock plans 48 Legacy Haynesville + 22 Western Haynesville wells for the rest of 2026 and is deploying its first 10,000-psi rig in the Western Haynesville this fall. As of **September 1, 2026 — two weeks ago** — Comstock entered a $450M drilling-cost venture with the Jones family (majority owner Jerry Jones) funding 85% of D&C costs on 18 Western Haynesville wells and 80% on 9 Legacy wells. Our TX master data already shows Comstock with 8 Haynesville permits split across Robertson/Leon/Harrison — this financing is what underwrites that book going forward.

**Expand Energy is spending more than half its 2026 budget here — and its new Western leasehold shows up as a name-level match in this week's filings.** Chesapeake's 2024 merger with Southwestern created Expand Energy, now the largest U.S. gas producer (7.48 Bcf/d in Q2 2026, 42.6% from the Haynesville). More than half of its full-year 2026 capex ($2.75–2.95B) is going to the Haynesville, funding a new 75,000+ net-acre Western Haynesville leasehold position (~$178M spent, 200+ potential locations). This is almost certainly the same activity behind the single largest lessee line in this week's export: **Expand Exploration LLC leased 42 tracts / 8,626.3 acres in Anderson County** — a county that sits *outside* both of `config.yaml`'s current Haynesville corridors ("DOXA/Sabine — East Texas" and "West Haynesville watch"). Worth flagging: leasehold is running well ahead of permits here — Anderson County TX master.csv shows only 5 small-operator permits total, none from Expand, Aethon, or Dorada Energy (the other big Anderson lessee, 18 leases). This is acreage assembly, not drilling activity, yet.

**Japanese/LNG-linked capital is the macro story of the cycle.** Between Mitsubishi's $7.5B Aethon deal and JERA's separate $1.5B acquisition of full ownership of the South Mansfield Haynesville asset in western Louisiana (closed February 2026), well over $9B of Japanese, LNG-linked capital has entered this play in eight months. It's visible directly in our own data too: **JERA Upstream America LLC** now carries 3 active LA Haynesville permits in Bossier. This is the single clearest instance this cycle of a macro trend (foreign LNG-linked capital chasing Gulf Coast feedgas) showing up concretely in county-level permit data.

**BPX (BP), by contrast, is pulling back.** BP is running only one rig in the Haynesville — a deliberate dry-gas-vs-liquids capital call even as it reports ~10% y/y capital-productivity gains there — consistent with BPX's comparatively modest 10-permit LA footprint (vs. Apex's 29, Expand's 21).

**Tracked-family roundup (from evidence, all corridors):** Sabine Energy carries the largest single TX Haynesville book (24 unspudded permits, Harrison/Panola/Rusk — the full DOXA/Sabine corridor) and 18 new leases this week (1,312.4 ac, Gregg/Smith/Harrison, filing as SABINE EAST TEXAS BASIN LLC); Comstock adds 16 leases (389.1 ac, Leon/Robertson).

**LA reporting-lag caveat:** the SONRIS free feed itself shows zero new/amended LA permits as of 9/13 ("source unchanged since 2026-09-13") — this is a known ~33-day average reporting lag on the LA REST layer, not a slowdown. The LA activity described above comes from `la/master.csv`'s deeper permit history and the Enverus lease export, both of which show the play very much alive.

## Eagle Ford / Eaglebine (Giddings)

This play is genuinely the quiet one this cycle, and the evidence says so plainly rather than needing to be padded: only **2 counties** (Atascosa 6, Karnes 5 permits) crack Texas's statewide top-12 for September, and the lease export tags just **6 leases EAGLEFORD** for the entire window (vs. 154 for Haynesville) — consistent with outside coverage describing Eagle Ford M&A as "muted relative to other U.S. shale basins" through this multi-year stretch.

**Where the activity actually is: Eaglebine/Giddings, and it's almost entirely two merging companies.** The Eagle Ford's live action right now is concentrated in the Eaglebine extension (Lee/Fayette/Washington/Austin/Colorado/Burleson — `config.yaml`'s "DLS/EOG — Giddings" corridor), where all 46 recent permits in our evidence were filed since July 2026. And the top two filers are about to become one company: **Magnolia Oil & Gas Operating (20 permits) is acquiring WildFire Energy (3 permits) for ~$4.06B**, adding 810,000 net acres and 53,000 boe/d to build a larger Eagle Ford/Austin Chalk South Texas position. Both names are drilling side-by-side in our Eaglebine data this exact month — once the deal closes, WildFire's active permit inventory folds directly into Magnolia's program. This is a rare case of watching consolidation happen in the permit stream before the deal even closes. **Ironbloom Operating** (1 permit) is `config.yaml`'s tracked "new Giddings entrant" and shows up right on schedule.

**ConocoPhillips runs the traditional Eagle Ford core through (at least) two legacy names.** ConocoPhillips's $22.5B all-stock acquisition of Marathon Oil closed November 2024 and boosted ConocoPhillips's Eagle Ford net-location count by 85%. **Marathon Oil EF LLC** (15 historical permits in our data) is now a ConocoPhillips subsidiary — as is **Burlington Resources O&G Co LP** (30 permits, ConocoPhillips's other long-standing Eagle Ford vehicle). Two names, one owner, both still active in the permit history.

**EOG remains the historical anchor** of the core Eagle Ford (46 permits, the largest single operator on file), consistent with EOG being the play's original developer and with `config.yaml`'s own framing of the Giddings corridor as "EOG-contiguous."

**ExxonMobil's absence from this play is itself a signal.** ExxonMobil is marketing roughly $1B of Eagle Ford position for sale as of early 2026 — that is exactly why the company does not appear anywhere in our Eagle Ford operator lists: it's a seller here, not a driller, right now.

**Other watch items:** Crescent Energy closed a ~$905M acquisition of Ridgemar Energy's Eagle Ford assets — neither name appears in our permit evidence yet, likely because the deal is too recent to show up in filings; worth checking upcoming digests for Crescent's entry. BPX (BP) is running an 8%-growth-in-2026 program built substantially on refracking decade-old Eagle Ford wells at roughly 2x original productivity thanks to improved completion technology — a redevelopment story rather than a new-leasehold story, matching BPX's steady-but-unspectacular 18-permit historical presence with no new leasing signal in this week's export.

## Permian Basin

The Permian dominates raw well counts this cycle — **10 of TX's statewide top-12 counties in September are Permian** (Martin 19, Reagan 18, Loving 13, Reeves 13, Crane 11, Midland 8, Glasscock 6, Borden 5, Yoakum 5, Gaines 4) — but the operator list is now a roster of the last three years' Permian mega-mergers.

**The two largest deals in Permian history sit directly underneath the top two counties.** ExxonMobil's $59.5B acquisition of Pioneer Natural Resources (closed May 2024) combined Pioneer's 850,000+ net Midland Basin acres with Exxon's own 570,000 net acres across the Midland and Delaware, an estimated 16 billion boe of combined resource, and a path to ~2 MMboe/d of Permian output by 2027 — Pioneer Natural Resources USA remains the single largest operator across our full TX master history (124 permits). Diamondback Energy's $26B merger with Endeavor Energy Resources (closed 2024) combined the 7th- and 9th-largest Permian producers into the 3rd-largest; Endeavor's legacy core position is in Martin County, this month's #1 county (19 permits) — Diamondback (110 historical permits, 11 this month) is now running that program.

**Double Eagle's build-scale-sell playbook is repeating itself in the permit data in real time.** Fort Worth's Double Eagle (Cody Campbell/John Sellers, backed by EnCap and Apollo) sold its Double Eagle IV Midland Basin assets to Diamondback for $4.08B ($3B cash + 6.9M shares), then immediately launched **Double Eagle V + Tumbleweed V** with $2.5B of fresh equity. Double Eagle V is already drilling: it registered new well-battery projects in Glasscock County, and our TX MTD digest shows **DE V Operating LLC with 6 permits this month, in Glasscock** — a direct match. **DE Central Operating LLC** (51 historical / 12 this month, TX's #2 operator in September) is very likely a related legacy Double Eagle vehicle still actively permitting under the same ownership group, though we could not confirm the exact corporate link via public sources — flagging as probable rather than confirmed.

**Occidental's multi-entity Permian footprint reflects its own two big buys.** Oxy's 2019 acquisition of Anadarko and its 2024 acquisition of CrownRock both show up as separate active filing entities: **Anadarko E&P Onshore LLC** (9 permits this month) and **Occidental Permian Ltd.** (10 permits this month) are both drilling under Oxy ownership, alongside **OxyRock Operating LLC** — TX's single largest filer this month at 14 permits, also Oxy-affiliated, running legacy assets including in Howard County. Three Oxy-linked names, one parent, three different acquisition vintages.

**Two operators whose county footprint is explained almost entirely by a single recent deal:** **Discovery Natural Resources**, a private Denver-backed operator, runs 1,195 wells on ~155,000 net acres concentrated in Reagan, Irion, Glasscock, and Crockett counties — which is exactly why Reagan is this month's #2 statewide county (18 permits) and Discovery shows 6 permits there; Discovery also picked up an EOG-to-Discovery air-permit transfer in Irion County in February 2026, a bolt-on signal of EOG rationalizing non-core acreage. **Ring Energy** made its Delaware Basin debut with 14,000 net acres in Culberson and Reeves counties for $75M (deal signed May 26, 2026) — Reeves is this month's #4 county (13 permits) and the single largest Permian lease county in this week's export (59 new leases, 10,764 acres), and Ring shows 4 fresh permits there.

**Central Basin Platform is about to get its own public vehicle.** SoftVest and Blackbeard signed a $2.24B merger to combine the Permian Basin Royalty Trust with Blackbeard's land and mineral assets (~111,000 net royalty acres, 68,000 surface acres), forming "PBT Land and Minerals, Inc.," primarily on the Central Basin Platform. Blackbeard Operating LLC is still actively drilling in the meantime — 7 permits this month, including a new four-well Davy Jones pad in Crane County — and the Central Basin Platform shows 16+2 fresh lease filings this week. Blackbeard is simultaneously drilling and about to become part of a public minerals company.

**Midstream is building ahead of this activity, which is itself a signal of sustained multi-year commitment:** ONEOK is acquiring Brazos Midstream's Midland Basin gathering system (~700 miles of gathering, 1.2 Bcf/d of processing capacity across seven core Midland counties once the Cassidy II plant comes online in Q3 2027), and Targa Resources is separately acquiring a Permian gathering & processing system for $1.25B.

**Tracked families:** none of the user's tracked `operator_families` show material direct Permian drilling this cycle except EOG (6 permits). Notably, **Firebird's own DOXA/Firebird corridor** (Ector/Winkler/Upton/Midland/Crane/Andrews) shows no Firebird permits in this month's top operators at all — third parties (Diamondback, Blackbeard, Ring, Discovery) are the ones actively filing around Firebird's own position in that corridor right now.

## Bottom line

The through-line across all three plays this cycle is capital re-rating gas over oil, and it's most visible in the Haynesville: well over $9B of Japanese/LNG-linked capital (Mitsubishi's Aethon buy, JERA's South Mansfield deal) has landed in the play in the last eight months, a hedge fund (Citadel/Apex) is now the most aggressive physical driller in the basin, and the largest U.S. gas producer (Expand Energy) is quietly assembling a brand-new 75,000+ acre position in a county — Anderson — that sits outside both of the corridors this pipeline currently tracks. Meanwhile the Permian and Eagle Ford stories are both about consolidation into fewer, larger hands (Exxon/Pioneer, Diamondback/Endeavor, ConocoPhillips/Marathon, Magnolia/WildFire) rather than new capital entering the play. If one thing here warrants a client conversation this week, it's the Anderson County leasehold buildout: real money (Expand, Aethon/Adamas, Dorada) is assembling acreage there right now, ahead of any permits, in a corridor `config.yaml` doesn't yet formally watch.
