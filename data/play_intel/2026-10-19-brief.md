**LA Haynesville: 155 permits, essentially flat from 154 on 10/15** — De Soto 61, Caddo 38, Red River 17, Bossier 15, Sabine 11, Bienville 5, Webster 4, Natchitoches 4. Leaders: Apex Natural Gas (31, tracked family), Expand Operating (26, tracked family), Comstock Oil & Gas–LA (15), BPX Operating (15), Adamas Energy (11, tracked family), WNR Haynesville Operating (8), EXCO Operating (8), JERA Upstream America (7), Ensight IV Energy Management (6), EGH Operating (5), Bayou State Oil (5), Grayrock Energy IV Operating (5).

**Apex Natural Gas (tracked family) is now confirmed, not just inferred, as the play's most active driller.** A fresh search surfaces direct Louisiana trade coverage: Apex is running **9 rigs in northwest Louisiana, up from 1 in March** — per the Louisiana Oil & Gas Association's most recent drilling report, the most active operator in the state, concentrated in De Soto Parish. That explains the shape of the data on both sides at once: Apex's 31 LA permits lead the play, and across the two most recent lease batches Apex is the single largest lessee by volume — 72 Caddo Parish leases and 33 Red River Parish leases in the 10/17 batch, 49 more Nacogdoches County (TX) leases in the 10/19 batch. Apex is the renamed Paloma Natural Gas, bought by Citadel (the hedge fund) for roughly $1.2 billion in February 2025; Citadel itself doesn't operate, but two December 2025 bolt-ons — $430 million of Haynesville acreage bought from Comstock, plus Azul's northwest Louisiana assets — gave Apex the operating base it's now drilling nine rigs against. [Shreveport-Bossier Advocate](https://www.shreveportbossieradvocate.com/business/apex-looks-to-sink-multiple-wells-in-north-louisiana-near-caddo/article_8c44530d-6ec6-4143-a759-ab158e0d2d2a.html), [World Oil (Citadel-Paloma)](https://worldoil.com/news/2025/3/19/citadel-hedge-fund-to-buy-1-billion-in-haynesville-natural-gas-assets/)

**Expand Operating/Expand Exploration/Expand Gulf Coast (tracked family, CHK+SWN) backs its #2 LA permit position with confirmed, large capital.** Expand Energy's reaffirmed 2026 guidance is 11–12 rigs company-wide and $2.75–$2.95 billion of capex, with **more than half of that going to Haynesville** — Q2 2026 alone put $851 million into the ground, more than half of it there. That matches the leasing: 17–20 Anderson County (TX) leases and a steady run of Natchitoches/Sabine/De Soto/Bossier Parish leases across both recent batches. [Oil & Gas Journal](https://ogj.com/general-interest/companies/article/55271312/expand-executives-earmark-300-million-to-add-capacity-for-2026), [SEC 8-K, Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000895126/000089512626000046/exe-ex_991x20260630x8kxpr.htm)

**JERA Upstream America's 7 Benson-field De Soto permits remain the clearest confirmed follow-through in the play on a closed foreign-capital deal.** JERA closed its $1.5 billion South Mansfield purchase in February 2026; the asset currently produces 500+ MMcf/d with 200 undeveloped locations, against a stated goal near 1 Bcf/d. [Offshore Technology](https://www.offshore-technology.com/newsletters/jera-closes-acquisition-south-mansfield-asset)

**A name that didn't fully exit: GEP Haynesville II.** The new Natchitoches permit flagged above belongs to GEP Haynesville II, LLC — the same entity that sold its South Mansfield interest to JERA *alongside* Williams in that deal. GEP is also still leasing on its own account (5 Sabine Parish leases in the 10/19 batch). Whatever GEP sold into the JERA deal, it evidently kept a separate Haynesville position it's still working — worth tracking as a name that looked like a clean seller but isn't fully gone.

**The LNG-linked foreign-capital roster in this play is now five deep, not four — Tokyo Gas is a confirmed name, not a rumor.** Tokyo Gas, through its TG Natural Resources unit, agreed to buy Rockcliff Energy for roughly $2.7 billion, aiming to roughly quadruple TGNR's Haynesville/Cotton Valley gas and NGL volumes (from ~330 MMcf/d toward 1,300 MMcf/d). That same Rockcliff — specifically "Rockcliff Energy Operating III" — is the #4 TX-core permit holder this cycle with 10 permits, all in San Augustine County, which lines up exactly with Rockcliff III's October 2025 purchase of Pine Wave Energy Partners' East Texas assets: 25 operated wells on ~29,000 net acres across San Augustine and Shelby Counties, ~100 MMcf/d net. [Hart Energy / Tokyo Gas-Rockcliff](https://www.hartenergy.com/exclusives/tokyo-gas-buy-rockcliff-energys-haynesville-assets-27b-207539). Combined with JERA (closed) and Mitsubishi/Adamas (announced Jan. 16, 2026 at ~$7.5B — $5.2B equity plus ~$2.33B net debt — still not confirmed closed in any primary source found today, though trade reporting treats it as done and the Adamas operating entity is already active in this cycle's own permit data), that's three confirmed-closed LNG-linked Japanese buyers now actually drilling in this play, plus Comstock-SOCAR pending. [Mitsubishi Corp release](https://www.mitsubishicorp.com/jp/en/news/release/2026/20260116002.html)

**Comstock-SOCAR still has not reached a definitive agreement, and the October 31 deadline is now 12 days out.** The only signed document remains the September 28 framework agreement — SOCAR acquiring a 20% non-op stake in Legacy Haynesville, 15%→7.5% in Western Haynesville, 15% of Comstock's Pinnacle Gas Services stake, for $1.65B cash, plus LNG-marketing cooperation. No definitive PSA found in any source checked today. [GlobeNewswire](https://energydigital.com/globenewswire/3369815). Independent of that deal's fate, **Comstock is the single largest lessee in this cycle's bigger batch**: 76 new Leon County (TX) leases in the 10/19 export, continuing to build the West Haynesville extension regardless of where SOCAR lands.

**A sixth, slower-building name: Caturus Energy (formerly Kimmeridge Texas Gas) and Black Stone Minerals.** The two signed a development partnership covering ~220,000 gross acres in the Shelby Trough/Haynesville-expansion area — East Texas, not South Texas — guiding to roughly 2 gross wells in 2026 ramping to ~12/year by year six. This is the same Caturus that closed the $950 million SM Energy Webb County (Eagle Ford) purchase in April; it's now simultaneously building a dry-gas Eagle Ford position and a long-dated Haynesville-expansion option, both explicitly tied to Gulf Coast LNG demand growth (Caturus's own guidance: ~15 Bcf/d of added LNG capacity by 2030). [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/natural-gas/101526-south-texas-producers-have-big-plans-to-develop-the-regions-mini-haynesville)

[SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001922446/000192244626000034/dec-20260430.htm)

## Bottom line

LNG-linked foreign capital keeps deepening its Haynesville footprint on money that's already closed — JERA, Mitsubishi/Adamas, and now confirmed Tokyo Gas/Rockcliff make three Japanese buyers actually drilling in this play (Rockcliff's San Augustine permits trace directly to its own October 2025 Pine Wave purchase) — while the two highest-dollar *pending* items, Comstock-SOCAR ($1.65B, 12 days to its Oct. 31 deadline) and Continental-FireBird (six-plus weeks past its guided close), remain unresolved for a second straight cycle, the latter with FireBird's entire corridor well inventory sitting fully unspudded in a pattern that fits an ownership-limbo story without proving one. Independent of deal timing, Apex Natural Gas supplies this cycle's clearest hard evidence of capital actually hitting the ground: confirmed via Louisiana trade press at 9 rigs running (up from 1 in March), it now leads both the LA permit count and the two most recent lease batches by a wide margin. Eagle Ford tells a cleaner, already-moving story — Crescent's $4.2B absorption of Devon's entire Karnes/DeWitt/Gonzales position closes the loop on Devon's post-Coterra-merger Permian pivot, with the frozen TX permit data confirming the exact footprint being sold. And underneath all three plays, two structural facts haven't changed: TX permitting is now 32 days into an RRC block with every county/operator figure here still "last known, not current," and the lease-filing feed's single largest unidentified name is no longer Midcontinent O&G or Cresta Exploration — it's GHO Energy, a ~170-lease Permian player with zero public footprint found anywhere in today's search.
