## Haynesville (Texas + Louisiana)

Full TX+LA history against the standard Haynesville footprint: **136 TX permits** (Harrison 29, Panola 29, San Augustine 26, Rusk 20, Shelby 10, Nacogdoches 9) plus **35 more in the West Haynesville extension** (Freestone 9, Robertson 7, Anderson/Leon/Madison 5 each) — **171 combined** — and **139 LA permits** in the tracked "RROG + DOXA — NW Louisiana Haynesville" corridor (De Soto 50, Caddo 36, Red River 15, Bossier 15, Sabine 11). **Flag on the LA corridor figure:** today's `market_brief.md` reports "no permits in the trailing 90 days" for that same corridor — but the brief's own Data Notes section states parish-level cohort analysis isn't built for LA yet, so that's a known coverage gap in the trailing-90-day view, not a literal stop in LA Haynesville activity; the 139-permit full-history figure above is the more trustworthy read.

**Today's lease surge landed hardest here: 288 new TX Haynesville leases plus 10 LA Haynesville leases — the single largest play-level slice of the entire 1,101-record batch**, and it breaks down cleanly by tracked family:

**Comstock Resources is both mid-transaction with a major LNG partner and the single largest lessee in today's entire batch.** Comstock and Azerbaijan's SOCAR signed a framework agreement September 28 advancing their $1.65B Haynesville partnership; the two companies now have until **October 31 — 26 days from this brief** — to sign a definitive purchase-and-sale agreement, with a close targeted for year-end 2026. On the ground: Comstock-tagged entities picked up **80 new leases, 13,020.2 acres** today, almost entirely in **Leon County** (the West Haynesville extension) under "Comstock Resources Inc et al" and "Comstock Oil & Gas LLC et al" — Leon alone shows 142 total leases today, 62 of them to an entity recorded as "Comstock O&G Louisiana." Comstock's existing permit book there (6 TX West-Haynesville permits, Freestone/Leon/Robertson, plus 15 LA permits, Bossier-centered) is the base this new leasehold builds on, and it's running in parallel with a second capital program — Comstock is separately reported doubling its Western Haynesville well count to 24 in 2026 (from 12 in 2025).

**Apex Natural Gas (Citadel-backed, ex-Paloma) is now confirmed as the play's single most active driller, and today it's building a TX leasehold position to match its LA permit lead.** Apex is reported running **11 active rigs and 6 frac crews** in the Haynesville — more combined activity than any other operator in the basin — with production up roughly 3x, from ~0.58 Bcf/d in January 2025 to ~1.73 Bcf/d by mid-2026. Apex already leads the LA permit corridor at 29 permits (the largest tracked-family filer in either state). Today's lease batch shows Apex picking up **58 new leases, 5,298.2 acres**, with **50 of Nacogdoches County's 53 total leases** — a TX-side expansion straight out of its LA base.

**Mitsubishi's Adamas Energy has fully closed its Aethon acquisition and is now the play's #2 gas producer, with the legacy "Aethon" brand still visibly alive in today's lease data.** The $7.5B deal (~$5.2B cash, ~$2.33B assumed debt) closed in July; Adamas, led by CEO Gordon Huddleston, now produces roughly 2.1 Bcf/d — second only to Expand Energy — and reporting describes it moving into an active-development phase (new well and air permits, pointing at compression/gathering/pipeline buildout next). The evidence shows the brand transition still mid-stream: today's 22 new Adamas-family leases (1,714.6 acres, all in **San Augustine County**) are recorded under **"Adamas Energy LLC," "Aethon III HV LLC,"** and **"Aethon United BR LP"** — three names, one buyer, in the same county on the same day. Adamas also carries 12 TX and 11 LA permits under its own name already.

**Expand Energy is pushing the hardest into the western edge of the TX corridor.** Company guidance calls for 11–12 rigs and $2.75–2.95B of 2026 capex, more than half directed at Haynesville. Today's lease batch shows Expand with **43 new leases, 14,577.2 acres** — the largest acreage pickup of any tracked family — concentrated almost entirely in **Houston County** (40 of its 42 total leases today) rather than its established LA base, where it still holds the #2 permit spot (26 permits, Caddo/Sabine-centered).

**Sabine Energy remains TX's largest standing Haynesville filer and picked up fresh leasehold on its home turf today.** Sabine Energy Inc. leads TX Haynesville permits outright (24 all-time, 17 trailing-90-day, Harrison/Panola/Rusk), and a related entity, "Sabine East Texas Basin LLC," added **24 new leases, 2,538.1 acres** today across Gregg, Harrison, Rusk, Smith and Upshur counties.

**Silver Hill Energy Partners just closed new capital and is actively rotating acreage between Haynesville and Eagle Ford.** Silver Hill closed an oversubscribed **$1.277B fifth investment fund** (announced mid-July) targeting Haynesville, Eagle Ford and Austin Chalk, and separately bought roughly 17,500 net acres of Eagle Ford/Austin Chalk leasehold from 1776 Energy. In the evidence, Silver Hill Energy Operating LLC carries 8 TX Haynesville permits (Shelby/Rusk) — a smaller book, but backed by real fresh capital flowing across both of the user's tracked plays at once.

**RFE Operating — not a tracked family, but now the dominant lessee in two of the corridor's core counties — is worth adding to the watch list.** RFE is a privately held Arklatex-basin operator based in Tyler, TX, with confirmed active drilling in Harrison County (the Shinnecock-Muirfield well, reported in March 2026). Today's data shows RFE-tagged entities ("RFE ETX III/IV," "RFE Overton LLC") taking **32 of Harrison County's 69 leases and 29 of Rusk County's 47 leases** — a bigger single-day leasing footprint in those two counties than any tracked name. RFE also carries 11 TX permits all-time (Rockcliff Energy Operating III, a related or adjacent entity, carries another 10) — this is real, building scale from a name the user isn't currently tracking.

**Diversified Energy (tracked family) closed its $245M East Texas deal on schedule and still doesn't show up as a filer.** The Sheridan Production acquisition (62 MMcfed, 397 Bcfe, ~75,000 net acres, contiguous with Diversified's existing East Texas position) closed in Q2 2026 as guided. Diversified's acquire-and-produce model means it isn't a permit or lease filer, so it stays invisible to both of this pipeline's primary feeds — still the standing watch item flagged last cycle, now confirmed closed rather than pending.

Haynesville rig activity, reported but not in this pipeline's own data: the play now reportedly holds **56 rigs, up more than 40% year-over-year**, and forecasters cited in recent reporting expect the Haynesville to supply more than two-thirds of incremental US gas production growth over the next two years.

TX history against the standard Eagle Ford footprint: **298 permits** (Webb 72, Dimmit 29, Atascosa/DeWitt/Karnes 28 each, Gonzales 27), led by EOG Resources (46), Burlington Resources (30), BPX (18), and a three-way tie at 15 — Javelin Energy Partners, Marathon Oil EF, FW Eagle Ford I, Lewis Petro Properties. The Eaglebine/Giddings extension adds **46 more** (Washington 29, Fayette 8, Lee 5), led by Magnolia (20) and Aethon Energy Operating (16).

**Crescent Energy is the consolidation story the last brief missed — it now owns or is buying three separate pieces of this corridor.** Crescent announced a **definitive agreement to acquire Vital Energy for approximately $3.1B** in an all-stock deal, on top of already having completed the **$905M acquisition of Ridgemar Energy's Eagle Ford assets**. Crescent's existing Eagle Ford arm, **Javelin Energy Partners** ("a Crescent Energy company," ~95 Mboe/d, operating the Eagleville and Briscoe Ranch fields), is already tied for the #4 permit filer in the whole TX Eagle Ford book (15 permits). None of Crescent's names show fresh leasing in today's batch, but between Javelin's existing position, Ridgemar now folded in, and Vital Energy pending, Crescent is building real scale here largely outside this pipeline's own lease feed.

**SM Energy sold its Eagle Ford position for $950M to Caturus Energy, and Caturus already has a toehold visible in today's data.** The sale is a clean basin-focus move for SM Energy (concentrating elsewhere); for Caturus, today's lease batch shows a single new lease in **Live Oak County** — a small but real, same-week signal that the buyer is already active on the ground. Live Oak is the single largest Eagle Ford county in today's leasing (60 leases, 219,563.7 acres), though the bulk of that acreage (56 leases) sits with an entity recorded as "Hydrocarbon Operating" that this search could not independently identify — flagged rather than guessed at.

**1776 Energy sold its Eagle Ford/Austin Chalk package to Silver Hill Energy Partners** (see Haynesville section above for Silver Hill's fund close) — a second buyer bringing fresh capital into the play from outside this pipeline's tracked-family list.

**Ensign Energy's Eagle Ford deal (closed June 2026) is now visible as live South Texas drilling.** "Ensign Natural Resources II LLC" carries 5 trailing-90-day permits in the DLS/EOG Giddings corridor (DeWitt/Gonzales) per today's market brief — a direct line from a confirmed 2026 acquisition to current permit activity.

**ConocoPhillips now runs the legacy Eagle Ford core under two names, unchanged from last cycle.** The $17.1B ConocoPhillips–Marathon Oil merger closed September 25; Burlington Resources (30 permits all-time, DeWitt-centered, 7 trailing-90-day) and Marathon Oil EF LLC (15 permits, Gonzales-centered, 5 trailing-90-day) are the same ultimate owner filing separately. Burlington picked up 4 fresh leases in DeWitt County today; Marathon showed no fresh leasing.

**Magnolia Oil & Gas's WildFire Energy acquisition has now closed — September 10, 2026 — and Magnolia has already issued post-close guidance.** The $4.06B deal added roughly 810,000 net acres across the Giddings corridor; Magnolia's Q4 2026 production guidance is now set at 159–161 Mboe/d combined. Magnolia remains the top tracked-family permit filer in the corridor (20 permits, Fayette/Lee/Washington, all unspudded 43–84 days per last cycle's intel-insight read) with no fresh leasing signal today.

**BP's look at Devon's Eagle Ford package remains a pass, and Devon's intent to sell hasn't changed.** BP evaluated but ultimately didn't proceed on Devon's South Texas Eagle Ford assets (reported September 24–25); BPX Operating Company holds a steady 18 Eagle Ford permits (4 trailing-90-day) with no new leasing — consistent with BP's broader $20B divestment program running through midstream stake sales rather than new upstream buys. Watch for a different Devon buyer to surface.

**Smaller names:** Sage Natural Resources (2 trailing-90-day permits, Fayette) and Ironbloom Operating (1 permit, Lee — the tracked "new Giddings entrant") both remain thin but present, with no fresh M&A signal found this cycle. Hawkwood Energy East Texas is a non-tracked name worth a passing note — it's the dominant lessee in Brazos County today (7 of 7 leases) and shows up again in Burleson.

**Today's leasing confirms the Permian — not Haynesville — pulled the single largest basin-level slice of the 1,101-record batch: 357 new leases** (Midland 224, Central Basin Platform 107, Delaware 26).

**A $2.24B merger directly involving one of the corridor's own permit filers is now in motion.** SoftVest, L.P. and Blackbeard Holdings, LLC signed a definitive Combination Agreement (July 28) to merge Blackbeard's oil, gas, mineral and land operations with the publicly traded **Permian Basin Royalty Trust**, creating a new company, **PBT Land and Minerals, Inc.** Blackbeard Operating, LLC — the entity that operates Waddell Ranch in Crane County, where the Trust holds a 75% net overriding royalty — is the same name carrying 11 trailing-90-day permits in the DOXA/Firebird corridor (Crane County) and 2 fresh leases today under "Blackbeard Resources." This is a live, named transaction sitting directly on top of evidence already in this pipeline.

**Continental Resources' acquisition of the user's own tracked Firebird family is now the most overdue item in this entire briefing.** Continental's deal to acquire FireBird Energy II (~32,000 boe/d, 147,000 net Permian resource acres, 54,000 net Midland Basin acres, 307 gross locations) was guided to close in **September 2026** when first announced in August. September has now ended and **no closing announcement has surfaced in two consecutive search cycles** (this one and the 10/1 brief's). `config.yaml` tags this family **"Firebird Energy"** directly. Continental (43 permits all-time) and Firebird Energy II (8 trailing-90-day permits, Ector County) are both still filing independently in the same corridor; Continental is also an active fresh lessee today — 28 of Midland County's 139 leases — independent of the pending deal. In today's lease batch, HighPeak still picked up 4 of Howard County's 32 leases — in its own Signal Peak area — the same week it's reportedly shopping itself.

**Devon–Coterra's merger of equals, closed in May, is directing the clearest capital-allocation signal in the basin.** The combined company's 2026 plan is ~$4.9B in capital spending, more than 60% of it in the Permian, on top of a separate $2.6B federal lease purchase (16,300 net acres, Lea/Eddy Counties, NM) completed in May. In TX evidence, Coterra Energy Operating Co. remains the #3 all-time Permian filer (109 permits, Culberson/Reeves-heavy — the Delaware Basin side) but still doesn't crack the trailing-90-day top 8, five months post-close — the same integration-pause pattern flagged last cycle. Separately, an entity recorded as "DE West Texas" shows up as a top-3 lessee in three different counties today (Martin, Glasscock, Reagan) — the name and Delaware/Midland county spread would fit a Devon affiliate, but this search could not confirm the entity's identity, so it's flagged rather than asserted.

**ConocoPhillips' Marathon Oil close (September 25) reaches into the Permian too.** The same $17.1B deal adds roughly 400 Permian Delaware locations to ConocoPhillips' book; COG Operating LLC — ConocoPhillips' existing Permian filer (46 permits, Andrews/Midland-centered, #3 trailing-90-day) — is where that inventory lands.

**Two long-closed megadeals still run under their legacy brand names, and one shows up again today as an active lessee.** Exxon's 2024 acquisition of Pioneer Natural Resources, and Diamondback's $26B merger with Endeavor Energy Resources, both continue to file under their pre-merger names (Pioneer 124 permits all-time; Diamondback E&P 110). Today's lease data adds a small, specific echo of the Diamondback-Endeavor deal: an entity named "Endeavor Energy" picked up 5 of Martin County's 27 leases today — the legacy name still actively leasing more than two years after its parent was absorbed.

**Occidental's two legacy entities remain independently active seven years after the Anadarko deal**, unchanged from last cycle: Anadarko E&P Onshore LLC and Occidental Permian Ltd. both sit in the all-time top 10 (Loving/Reeves-heavy), same ultimate parent, two names.

## Bottom line

The dominant fact of this cycle is that the lease-filing feed just woke up after thirteen dry days with the largest batch this pipeline has ever recorded — 1,101 new leases — and the Permian, not Haynesville, took the largest single slice of it (357 leases vs. Haynesville's 298), even though Haynesville is where this cycle's tracked-family M&A is most active (Comstock-SOCAR's October 31 deadline, Apex's confirmed 11-rig/6-frac-crew lead, Adamas's visible brand transition, Expand's push into Houston County). Layered against that: at least three deals directly touching names already in this pipeline's own evidence are unresolved or just resolved — Continental-FireBird is now a month past its guided close with no confirmation found (the single item most worth a direct check with the client), Comstock-SOCAR has a hard 26-day deadline to a definitive agreement, and the Blackbeard-Permian Basin Royalty Trust merger ($2.24B) sits directly on top of the Crane County permit data already in `market_brief.md`. Eagle Ford added a consolidation story this pipeline hadn't caught before — Crescent Energy rolling up Javelin, Ridgemar and now a $3.1B bid for Vital Energy — while Magnolia-WildFire moved from pending to closed (Sept 10) in the four days since the last brief. The structural caveat underneath all of it is unchanged and now chronic: TX permit visibility has been frozen at a 31-day-old snapshot since RRC's IP block took effect on day 19 and counting, and this pipeline's own rig-activity feed has never worked even once — real activity (closings, framework-agreement deadlines, fresh leasing) is outrunning what this pipeline can confirm same-day, and today's lease-feed resumption is the first sign in two weeks that any part of the gap is closing rather than widening.
