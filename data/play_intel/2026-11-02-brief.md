## Bottom line

TX RRC stayed fully dark for a fourth straight Play Intel cycle — 59 days behind, every permit count across all three plays identical to 10/29 — so once again every genuine signal this cycle came from Louisiana's live permit feed (now five days stale itself, since no daily job has committed past 10/30) and the corporate layer. Three things stand out. First, the Comstock–SOCAR deadline that the last cycle flagged as "two days out" has now passed with no definitive agreement found in a direct search — the deal may simply be running past its own self-imposed, explicitly-hedged target, but a missed deadline is a different fact than a pending one and should be tracked as such going into whatever Comstock says next. Second, this cycle re-verified rather than recycled three of the basin's largest 2026 corporate events directly against SEC filings — Devon–Coterra ($58B, closed May 7), SM Energy–Civitas (closed January 30), and Magnolia–WildFire ($4.06B, closed September 10) — all three remain the load-bearing explanations for names that otherwise look odd in the raw permit data (Coterra filing in TX Permian, Aethon and Adamas both appearing live in different plays, Magnolia's leaderboard position understating its real post-close size). Third, the leasing data is still surfacing the same cluster of unverified, fund-named entities — Lone Peak Energy, Incline Permian III, Black Swan Permian II, and Espuela Energy in the Permian, Bison Energy Group in the TX Haynesville — for a second or more consecutive cycle with zero corroborating corporate identity found in search; at this point the absence of a trail is itself the finding, and a direct Texas Secretary of State / RRC operator-registration lookup (outside this pipeline's current data) would resolve it faster than another web search cycle. Underneath all three plays, the confirmed foreign-capital threads keep accumulating rather than resolving: JERA's closed $1.5B Haynesville buy, Mitsubishi's closed Aethon/Adamas transaction spanning both Haynesville and Eagle Ford, and now HighPeak's reported (unconfirmed) $450M Indonesian-backed preferred placement in the Permian — three separate basins, three separate foreign counterparties, the same underlying story of outside capital buying into U.S. shale gas and oil ahead of LNG and power demand.
