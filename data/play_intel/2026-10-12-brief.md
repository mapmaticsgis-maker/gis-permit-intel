## Eagle Ford / Eaglebine (Giddings)

Permit picture unchanged from last cycle: **287 TX-core permits** (Webb 72, Dimmit 29, Atascosa/DeWitt/Karnes 28 each, Gonzales 27) led by EOG Resources (44), Burlington Resources (30), BPX (18), Javelin Energy Partners (15), Marathon Oil EF (15), Lewis Petro Properties (15); plus **44 more** in the Eaglebine/Giddings extension, led by Magnolia (20) and Aethon Energy Operating (16).

**The headline for this cycle: Devon Energy is exiting the Eagle Ford entirely, and Crescent Energy is the buyer — resolving the "unresolved Devon exit" thread flagged the last two cycles running.** Confirmed via Crescent's own October 8 SEC 8-K and press release, cross-checked against JPT/SPE and Oil & Gas Journal coverage: **Crescent Energy agreed to acquire Devon's entire Eagle Ford position for $4.22 billion in cash per the SEC filing** (Crescent's own press release frames it as ~$3.85B net of adjustments) — roughly **89,000 net acres, 97% operated, in Karnes, DeWitt, and Gonzales Counties**, carrying ~68 Mboe/d of net production and 600+ Tier 1 locations. Crescent identifies ~$140M in annual synergies and has a $2.0B JPMorgan bridge facility backing the cash deal; closing is guided for Q4 2026 or early 2027. This is the same Devon-subsidiary transaction the 10/8 brief flagged as "not yet confirmed as Eagle Ford-specific" from an earlier 424B3 reference — now confirmed, and it is Eagle Ford. Devon-owned mineral interests are also being folded into Crescent Royalties. **DeWitt and Gonzales are both counties where BPX, EOG, and Marathon Oil EF already carry permits in this corridor's top-filer list** — Crescent now becomes a direct neighbor/counterparty to all three on the ground, not just a portfolio statistic.

This also completes the picture on Crescent's broader Eagle Ford roll-up, already well-documented: the $905M Ridgemar Energy purchase and the December 2025 Vital Energy close (all-stock, ~73.3M shares, $890M debt repaid) are both already in place. Crescent's Javelin Energy Partners arm remains tied for #4 TX-core permit filer (15 permits); no fresh leasing from any named Crescent entity in today's lease file.

**SM Energy's prior divestiture context is unchanged**, carried forward from last cycle: Caturus Energy (rebranded from Kimmeridge Texas Gas) closed its $950M Galvan Ranch purchase from SM on April 30 — ~61,000 net acres, ~260 wells, centered on Webb County — as part of SM's post-Civitas-merger divestiture mandate (at least $1B targeted). No fresh Caturus leasing in today's file.

**Magnolia's WildFire Energy integration remains the established baseline** — 20 TX-core permits (tracked family, top filer in the corridor) — with no new leasing signal today; the 16-well Clay Creek Aethon program noted in last cycle's intel-insight pull (Washington County, all unspudded, 39–47 days aging as of 10/11) continues to sit in the same queue.

**EOG Resources** (tracked family) shows light but live leasing this cycle: 3 new leases (DeWitt 1, Lavaca 2, totaling 22.1 acres) in today's file — small dollar figures, but confirms continued ground-level activity in its existing DeWitt/Lavaca/Gonzales core alongside its 44-permit lead in the play.

**Smaller tracked names, unchanged:** Sage Natural Resources (4 permits, Fayette/Lee) and Ironbloom Operating (1 permit, Lee) show no new leasing in today's file; 1776 Energy's January sale to Silver Hill and Silver Hill's oversubscribed $1.277B fifth fund remain the standing context, with no update found today.

## Bottom line

The single most important development this cycle is Crescent Energy's $4.2B agreement to buy Devon's entire Eagle Ford position (announced Oct. 8) — it converts a two-cycle-old "unresolved Devon exit" question into a confirmed, named, dated transaction that reshapes who EOG, BPX, and Marathon Oil EF are now operating alongside in Karnes/DeWitt/Gonzales, and it's the clearest evidence this cycle that Eagle Ford consolidation (Crescent, SM/Civitas, Caturus, Magnolia/WildFire) is still actively compounding rather than settling. Set against that, both permit masters this pipeline depends on are frozen — TX for a chronic, unresolved reason (the RRC IP block, now 38 days stale on issue date) and LA for a fixable one (a git push race that has already cost three real Apex permits a committed home) — so this cycle's "what's new" signal comes almost entirely from the lease-filing feed and today's web research, not from permit counts. Two items remain more overdue than they should be: Comstock-SOCAR's October 31 deadline is inside three weeks with still only a framework agreement on record, and Continental-FireBird has now missed its guided September close by a full cycle with no confirmation found anywhere. The Permian's noticeably thin lease-filing activity this specific cycle, against Haynesville's and Eagle Ford's active pace, is worth watching next cycle rather than reading as a trend from one day's file.
