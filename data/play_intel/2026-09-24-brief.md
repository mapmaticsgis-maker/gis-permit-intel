## Haynesville (Texas + Louisiana)

Still the loudest play in the evidence. Combined TX+LA permit history shows **298 Haynesville permits** (167 TX / 131 LA) against the standard Haynesville county footprint, and the last live lease-filing snapshot (9/21) tagged **297 of 720 new leases (41%)** to Haynesville TX+LA — both figures unchanged from last cycle since neither source has moved.

**Comstock's SOCAR deal is a signed letter of intent, not yet closed — worth being precise about that distinction.** Comstock announced on 9/1 a $1.65B strategic partnership in which SOCAR (Azerbaijan's state oil company) would pay for a 20% non-operated interest in Comstock's Legacy Haynesville assets and 15% of Western Haynesville, alongside a separate $450M Jones-family drilling venture (85%/80% D&C cost funding on 18 Western + 9 Legacy wells, effective 9/1). Comstock's own materials describe the SOCAR piece as a **letter of intent expected to close by year-end**, bringing pro forma net debt down to ~$1.5B from $3.1B. Comstock's TX permit book remains concentrated in Harrison/Panola/Rusk (8 permits per the full county sweep, 24 when the tracked-family alias including LA is counted), and yesterday's intel-insight pad read shows Comstock's actual in-corridor exposure is smaller than headline counts suggest: only 2 of its 7 TX client-watch permits (BRYANT RAINS BMP/EB, Harrison, 65–68 days) sit in the DOXA/Sabine corridor itself — the other 5 belong to the separate West Haynesville watch list (3 Leon, 1 Robertson, 1 Freestone).

**Apex (Citadel-backed) remains the play's dominant lessee and, per the Haynesville's own rig tallies, one of its most active drillers.** Citadel bought Paloma Natural Gas for $1.2B in Feb 2025, renamed it Apex Natural Gas, then paid Comstock $430M in Dec 2025 for additional LA/TX acreage; trade coverage has Apex running as many as 13–14 rigs and ramping from near-zero in 2024 to the basin's most aggressive build-out. That tracks with our evidence: Apex leased 183 tracts (2,735.9 ac) in the last live digest — 147 of Caddo Parish's 165 total leases and all 18 of Nacogdoches County's — and carries 29 LA Haynesville permits (Caddo/Red River-heavy), the #1 operator in that state's tracked history. Basin-wide, Haynesville rig counts are running in the mid-50s to low-60s per weekly Baker Hughes-tracked reads through 9/18 — essentially flat week over week, still well up from the 2024 trough that predates Apex's build-out.

**Mitsubishi's Aethon deal is closed, and the Aethon→Adamas rename is now visible in permits, not just leases.** Mitsubishi's ~$7.5B (per Mitsubishi's own release; other coverage frames the cash-plus-assumed-debt structure as ~$5.2B equity/$2.33B debt) acquisition of Aethon's Haynesville business closed 7/15/2026; Aethon retains a right to buy back up to 25% of the upstream/midstream assets and is repositioning as an investment manager. `config.yaml` already tags this as **"Adamas (ex-Aethon, Mitsubishi)."** TX master now shows **Adamas Energy LLC with 14 permits** (San Augustine 11, Robertson 2, Nacogdoches 1) alongside 11 in LA (Bienville, Red River, Bossier, Natchitoches) — the rename has fully propagated to the Haynesville book on both sides of the state line.

**Expand Energy's Western Haynesville bet keeps advancing on the ground, separate from its steady leasing pace.** Expand (Chesapeake+Southwestern) built a 75,000+ net-acre Western Haynesville position for ~$178M (~$117M cash, ~$29M capital carry through 2027) and has since drilled a vertical appraisal well and is planning its first horizontal test there — the company frames it explicitly as a bid to be the sub-play's lowest-cost producer. The 9/21 lease digest showed 20 new Expand leases (441.7 ac, De Soto/Caddo), and yesterday's pad read adds one fresh in-corridor pad: a 3-well Caddo Parish filing (HA RA SUMM/ARK, issued 9/15, 8 days old, no numbering gap) inside the tracked RROG+DOXA NW Louisiana corridor.

**JERA's closed South Mansfield deal still shows up as GEP Haynesville leasehold — a name outside the tracked list worth adding.** JERA's $1.5B South Mansfield acquisition (closed Feb 2026) sits behind GEP Haynesville's continued presence as the #2 lessee in Natchitoches Parish (11 leases) plus positions in Sabine and Red River Parishes in the 9/21 digest — unchanged from last cycle since no fresher lease data has landed.

**Sabine Energy carries the single largest unspudded backlog in the entire TX book.** Per yesterday's intel-insight read: 23 unspudded Sabine permits spread across Panola (HILL CV, SOCAGEE CREEK, WYATT), Harrison (BOLDING-STRONG, BONNER NORTH), and Rusk (HELVENSTON, COLEMAN), aging 21 to **495 days** — the Panola HILL CV pair has sat since May 2025. Sabine remains TX's #1 Haynesville filer by raw count (24 permits, corridor-wide).

**BPX (BP) stays the quiet, steady name** — 10 LA Haynesville permits on file, no new leasing signal, consistent with BP running a single Haynesville rig and prioritizing capital productivity over new acreage.

## Eagle Ford / Eaglebine (Giddings)

Quieter by volume — 368 Eagle Ford/Eaglebine permits in the TX master history against 113 tagged leases (48 EAGLEFORD + 65 EAGLEBINE) in the last live digest — consistent with outside coverage describing Eagle Ford M&A as structurally muted relative to the Permian.

**Magnolia's WildFire acquisition has closed — new and confirmed this cycle.** Last cycle flagged the deal as "essentially closing now"; it's now official: **Magnolia Oil & Gas announced completion on September 10, 2026.** WildFire owners received 32.2 million shares of Magnolia Class A stock, and Magnolia assumed WildFire's $600M of notes due 2029. The deal adds ~810,000 net acres (including a sand mine supplying ~80% of Magnolia's annual sand needs and 500+ miles of gas-gathering pipe) and pushes Magnolia's combined Giddings position past 1.25M net acres across the Austin Chalk, Eagle Ford, and Woodbine. Magnolia carries 20 permits in the tracked DLS/EOG Giddings corridor (Fayette/Lee/Washington) — per yesterday's aging read, all 20 are still unspudded (a 6-well CYCLONE pad in Fayette at 61–65 days; MAC ARTHUR and TRUMAN clusters in Washington at 36–42 days; singles at 76–77 days) — meaning the newly-combined company inherits a substantial pre-close DUC-adjacent inventory on day one.
