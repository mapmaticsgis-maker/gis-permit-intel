INTEL INSIGHT — October 27, 2026

Quick pull: TX sits at 2,037 rows in master, LA at 234. Zero new permits surfaced in either state's pull today, and no fresh spuds posted against the snapshot. That is not the same story in both states, though — read on, because what it means in Texas is materially different from what it means in Louisiana.

TEXAS: THE FEED IS BLIND, NOT QUIET

The TX master's newest visible issue date is September 4 — 53 days behind today. That lines up almost exactly with the known RRC IP block (confirmed by phone with TXRRC on September 17, per config) that's been sitting on both the daf420 feed and the W-1 subscription pull ever since. In other words, today's "zero new permits" in Texas isn't a slow day in the field — it's six-plus weeks of RRC not letting us see anything past early September. Every "unspudded" and "aging" figure below for Texas should be read as "at least this old, as of our last confirmed look," not as a live read on what operators are actually doing on the ground right now. Some of this backlog may already be moving; we're simply flying without the instrument until RRC lifts the block.

That caveat aside, the standing inventory itself is worth walking through, because it's a real and sizable picture of permitted-but-undrilled capacity sitting across every one of our tracked East Texas and Permian corridors.

PERMIAN — FIREBIRD'S 12-WELL BACKLOG SITTING IDLE IN ECTOR

Firebird Energy II LLC — the operator the DOXA/Firebird Permian corridor (Ector, Winkler, Upton, Midland, Crane, Andrews) is built around — is carrying 12 open permits in Ector County, all still showing unspudded as of our last look: the six-well Pocket Pair complex (2715 A/B/C and 2722 A/B/C), issued July 1 and now 118 days old, and the six-well Jake P complex (A through F), issued in three waves between August 14 and August 28, running 60 to 74 days old. Twelve permitted laterals, zero visible spuds. Given this is the corridor's anchor operator, that's the single most consequential TX number in this brief — and it's exactly the kind of thing the RRC blackout could be masking real activity on.

EAST TEXAS HAYNESVILLE — SABINE'S TAIL IS THE LARGEST IN THE BOOK

Sabine Energy carries 23 open client-watch permits across Rusk, Panola, and Harrison — more than any other tracked name in Texas — running from 55 days up to 529 days old. The two oldest, Hill CV A and B in Panola, were issued back in May 2025. Worth a technical note: a chunk of that 23 is double-counted wellbores — Sabine routinely files both a CV and an HS (or OU) permit on the same hole (Coleman A/B, Helvenston C/D, Bonner North A-D all show this pattern), so the real distinct-wellbore count is closer to 17, not 23. Either way, it's a large, broad-based permitted inventory — Socagee Creek A-D (4 wells, issued late March through late May, 159-214 days old) and the Bonner North cluster (4 wells, 57-92 days old) are the two most recent, most coherent pads in that tail. Comstock Oil & Gas adds another 7 aging permits spread thinner across Harrison, Leon, Robertson, and Freestone — straddling both the East Texas Haynesville corridor and the separate West Haynesville watch list — nothing pad-scale, just a steady trickle of singles and twos.

GIDDINGS & EASTERN EAGLE FORD — A MIXED PICTURE, AND ONE PERMIT WORTH FLAGGING SEPARATELY

Magnolia Oil & Gas has the second-largest tail in the state: 20 open permits across Washington, Lee, and Fayette, aging 70-111 days — Big Al Seven/Nine/Eleven and Means C in Washington, the six Cyclone locations (Two, Four, Six, Eight, Ten, Twelve) in Fayette, Mac Arthur Unit and four Truman wells rounding it out. Nothing here reads as stalled so much as a large, patient inventory typical of a contiguous Eagle Ford operator running development-mode economics rather than racing to drill out every permit on issuance.

Aethon Energy — tracked under the Adamas family (ex-Aethon/Mitsubishi) — has 16 open permits in Washington County alone, all against the Clay Creek East and West units, issued in a tight window between August 25 and September 2, ages 55-63 days. The well numbers here (259, 525, 630, etc.) are unit-allocation numbers from a mature field, not a fresh drilling sequence, so this reads as routine permitting on an established unit rather than a new push.

Marathon Oil EF — the family tagged for the "eastern EF push" — is worth flagging for contrast rather than scale: 9 open permits in Gonzales and DeWitt, but they split cleanly into two stories. Barnhart (EF) 205H/206H/207H plus the Barnhart-HFS SA9/SA10 pair — five wells issued mid-to-late July, 98-103 days old — look like genuine recent activity consistent with the "push" label. But Cherry Bergey Selman SA4 and SA1, in the same Gonzales corridor, have sat unspudded for 683 days (issued December 2024) — well before the RRC blackout even started. Those two aren't "aging," they're dead or shelved; they shouldn't be read against the rest of Marathon's live inventory. Separately, EOG — the corridor's namesake operator — has only a single straggler (Roosevelt F 6H, 476 days, Gonzales), which says the rest of EOG's program in this corridor is otherwise current. Sage Natural Resources (Albert Luecke North/South in Lee, Holliday EF 1H/2H in Fayette) and Ironbloom (a single well, Nelda 1H in Lee) round out the corridor with small, unremarkable positions.

LOUISIANA: FLAT TODAY, BUT NOT QUIET UNDERNEATH

Zero new LA permits in today's pull is expected, not a signal — the REST layer here typically lags the actual permit date by roughly a month before it becomes visible, so a clean zero is normal background noise, not evidence nothing is happening in the field. The most recent issue date we can actually trust is October 21 (strip out one clearly bad record — a "Kansas & Gulf Co." well in Red River Parish carrying an issue date of February 2031, obviously a data-entry error, not a real future permit).

The pad activity sitting in that most-recent window is genuinely interesting. Three different operators — BPX Operating, EXCO Operating, and WNR Haynesville Operating — each filed their own two-well pad in De Soto Parish within a single week (October 6-13): Matthews Tr7-18-19HC, Hall 1, and Petro-Hunt 13-12HC respectively. Three separate companies drilling in the same parish inside the same week is worth watching as a read on how much active competition for location is still happening in De Soto specifically. Separately, Denbury Onshore filed a five-well batch on October 16 against its long-running Delhi Holt Bryant unit in Richland Parish — outside our tracked corridor, and the well numbering (169-4, 179-3, 179-4, 138-1, 139-1) reads as unit-allocation permitting on a mature CO2-flood unit rather than new drilling. Gulf Coast Sequestration's two-well Minerva South filing in Cameron Parish (October 21) and Shirley Wells OC's two-well Shirley pad in La Salle Parish (October 20) are both outside the NW Louisiana Haynesville corridor as well and read as routine, unrelated activity.

On the client-watch side, four tracked families are carrying real, aging inventory in the Haynesville corridor: Apex Natural Gas leads with 15 open permits across Caddo and Red River (20-151 days old) — the four-well Kuhn pad and five-well CRDL pad in Caddo are 151 and 139 days old, respectively, while the two-well Brewer pad in Red River is fresh at 20 days. Expand Operating (the CHK/SWN/Indigo rollup) is running the widest footprint — 13 permits spread across Caddo, Red River, Natchitoches, and Sabine, 28-150 days old — more geographically scattered than deep in any one parish. Adamas has 11 open permits across Bienville, Bossier, Natchitoches, and Red River (69-146 days), and Comstock Oil & Gas–LA adds 5 more in Bossier and Sabine (28-124 days). None of this moved today, but it's a meaningful standing inventory that's going to need attention whenever it starts converting to spuds.
