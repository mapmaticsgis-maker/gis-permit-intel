Activity is concentrated in Andrews, Ector, and Midland; Upton carries only Apache's one pad; Winkler and Crane show zero pad activity and zero client-watch entries today — genuinely quiet ground this cycle, not a data gap.

By corridor well count, Pioneer Natural Resources leads at 22 wells across 8 pads, but the shape says infill, not a single campaign — one clean 8-well UNIVERSITY pad in Andrews (no gaps) plus seven two-well pairs scattered through Midland (BROOKS-CRESPI, CRESPI-GOLLADAY, DONALD L HUTT, SPOONBILL-DONOVAN, TURNER, WINKLEMAN-DONOVAN, and ODANIEL-FLOYD, which is missing wells 103 and 104 between 102H and 105H). Diamondback is also running the single largest pad anywhere in today's pull — STANTON 28-16, 14 wells permitted in a tight two-day window (August 11-12) — but that pad sits in Martin County, which is not one of the six DOXA/Firebird counties, so it counts as adjacent-basin color, not a corridor hit, and isn't in the 18 above.

Also worth registering, outside any tracked corridor: EOG's CODIGO pad in Webb County — 12 wells permitted August 11-17 with no gaps, EOG's biggest single pad in this pull. EOG is a tracked family, but Webb isn't inside any of the five corridor definitions in config, so this is basin color for a name we already track, not a corridor signal.
